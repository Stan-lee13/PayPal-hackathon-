## 1. Key findings (top 10)

1. **The US federal consumer-finance regulator largely stood down in 2025.** The CFPB was ordered to halt work in February. It dropped its Zelle suit on 4 March. Congress used the Congressional Review Act to repeal the overdraft rule and the "larger participant" digital-payments rule on 9 May. The bureau's funding cap was cut from 12% to 6.5% of Fed operating expenses on 4 July. More than 1,400 staff received layoff notices, and the courts were still fighting over them in December. VERIFIED ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/15/cfpb-staff-layoffs-can-proceed-appeals-court-rules/) [F]; [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/09/trump-big-beautiful-bill-slashes-cfpb-funding-what-it-means.html) [F]; [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/cfpb-drops-zelle-lawsuit-in-latest-reversal-under-trump-administration-204639032.html) [F]).
2. **Prudential policy moved toward deregulation and "fair access".** The OCC (March) and the Fed (June) dropped reputational risk from bank exams. A debanking executive order followed on 7 August, and on 10 December the OCC said the nine largest banks had restricted lawful industries. The enhanced supplementary leverage ratio (eSLR) was loosened on 25 November, freeing about $13bn of holding-company tier 1 capital. Bank mergers closed in about 4 months on average, the fastest since 1990. VERIFIED ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-4.html) [F]; [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20250623a.htm) [F]; [White House EO 14331](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/08/guaranteeing-fair-banking-for-all-americans/) [F]; [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/news/speeches/2025/final-rule-modify-enhanced-supplementary-leverage-ratio) [F]; [PYMNTS/FT](https://www.pymnts.com/bank-regulation/2025/bank-merger-approval-rates-hit-35-year-high/) [F]).
3. **Stablecoins entered the regulated perimeter.** The GENIUS Act was signed on 18 July: 1:1 high-quality liquid reserves, no issuer-paid interest, and Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) coverage. Exchanges are not barred from paying yield. Circle IPO'd at $31 on 5 June and closed day one up 168%. On 12 December the OCC conditionally approved national trust charters for Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets and Paxos. VERIFIED ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-08-20_IN12553_0b329134f6be0cc12f78017358552f4053d52c5e.html) [F]; [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/07/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-signs-genius-act-into-law/) [F]; [IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/the-ipo-buzz-circle-internet-group-prices-upsized-ipo-at-31-3-above-range/) [F]; [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/382379/ripple-bitgo-and-paxos-secure-conditional-approval-for-us-banking-charters) [F]).
4. **Bank failures were negligible, but credit-fraud "cockroaches" appeared.** Only two tiny US banks failed (Pulaski Savings, January; Santa Anna National, June), and fraud was a factor in both. In autumn, Tricolor (liquidated 10 September) and First Brands (Chapter 11, up to $2.3bn of unpaid loans) exposed fraud in asset-based and private credit. JPMorgan took a $170m charge, Zions a $50m one, and Western Alliance sued over about $100m. VERIFIED ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/resolutions/bank-failures/in-brief/2025) [F]; [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/jpmorgan-dimon-tricolor-chargeoff-earnings/802740/) [F]; [AML Intelligence](https://www.amlintelligence.com/2025/10/news-us-banking-stocks-hit-after-two-lenders-reveal-fraud-disclosures/) [F]).
5. **Fintech public markets reopened, but at reset valuations.** eToro listed in May. Chime listed in June at about $11.6bn, against a $25bn peak in 2021. Klarna listed in September at about $15.2bn, against $45bn in 2021. Revolut, by contrast, hit $75bn in a private secondary sale while still lacking a full UK banking licence. VERIFIED ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/klarna-raises-usd-137-billion-in-us-ipo) [F]; [RTÉ/Reuters](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2025/1124/1545556-revolut-valued-at-75-billion) [F]; Chime [S]).
6. **UK conduct regulation turned to redress.** The first year of mandatory APP-scam reimbursement returned 88% of money claimed (£112m in 9 months). The Supreme Court's motor-finance ruling on 1 August led to an FCA redress scheme it costed at "unlikely much lower than £9bn" and up to £18bn. Monzo was fined £21.1m for AML failings. The PSR found that Visa and Mastercard had raised scheme and processing fees by more than 25% in real terms. VERIFIED ([PSR](https://www.psr.org.uk/news-and-updates/latest-news/news/one-year-on-impact-of-app-reimbursement-on-victims/) [F]; [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-consult-motor-finance-compensation-scheme) [F]; [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-monzo-21m-failings-financial-crime-controls) [F]; [PSR](https://www.psr.org.uk/news-and-updates/latest-news/news/psr-finds-market-isn-t-working-well-for-card-scheme-and-processing-services/) [F]).
7. **The EU built "payment sovereignty" and fraud liability.** DORA applied from 17 January. On 27 November a provisional PSD3/PSR deal made payment providers (PSPs) liable for weak fraud controls, mandated name/IBAN matching, required full refunds for bank-impersonation scams, and made online platforms liable to PSPs. The ECB moved the digital euro to its next phase on 30 October, aiming at 2029. Wero went live for e-commerce in Germany in November. EEA payment fraud reached €4.2bn in 2024. VERIFIED ([EP](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251121IPR31540/payment-services-deal-more-protection-from-online-fraud-and-hidden-fees) [F]; [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2025/html/ecb.pr251030~8c5b5beef0.en.html) [F]; [ECB/EBA](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2025/html/ecb.pr251215~e133d9d683.en.html) [F]; [Deutsche Bank](https://www.db.com/news/detail/20251217-deutsche-bank-launches-wero-for-more-simple-and-sovereign-digital-payments-in-europe?language_id=1) [F]).
8. **Payment infrastructure modernised, but the G20 targets are slipping.** FedNow settled 8.41m payments in 2025 (+459%). Swift's MT/MX coexistence ended on 22 November. Nexus Global Payments was incorporated in April. Pix Automático launched on 16 June. The FSB said the 2027 cross-border targets are "unlikely" to be met. A US Section 301 probe opened on 15 July targets Brazil's "government-developed electronic payment services". VERIFIED ([FRB Services](https://www.frbservices.org/resources/financial-services/fednow/quarterly-volume-value-stats) [F]; [FSB](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P091025-1.pdf) [F]; [Federal Register/USTR](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-07-18/pdf/2025-13498.pdf) [F]).
9. **Agentic commerce became the main battleground for networks and wallets.** Visa Intelligent Commerce launched on 30 April and Google AP2 on 16 September (60+ partners). PayPal adopted OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol on 28 October and integrated Mastercard Agent Pay on 27 October. VERIFIED ([Visa](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.21361.html) [F]; [Google](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol) [F]; [PayPal](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2025-10-28-OpenAI-and-PayPal-Team-Up-to-Power-Instant-Checkout-and-Agentic-Commerce-in-ChatGPT) [F]).
10. **Africa had a credibility year, though funding for fintech fell.**
    - Nigeria and South Africa left the FATF grey list on 24 October.
    - Nigeria's account ownership rose from 45.3% (2021) to 63.3% (2024) in Global Findex 2025.
    - Ethiopia opened banking to foreign banks on 25 June.
    - Mobile money reached 2.1bn accounts and moved $1.68tn in 2024.
    - Equity funding for African fintech fell 43% in 2025.

    VERIFIED ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/10/24/four-african-countries-taken-off-global-money-laundering-grey-list) [F]; [World Bank Little Data Book](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/be6615202d1f08a25855c8ac2d615122-0050012025/related/Little-Data-Book-2025-Web.pdf) [F]; [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/06/26/ethiopia-opens-banking-sector-to-foreign-investors/) [F]; [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/sotir/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/The-State-of-the-Industry-Report-2025_English.pdf) [F]; [Partech](https://partechpartners.com/africa-reports/2025-africa-tech-venture-capital-report) [F]).

## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 US: the CFPB retreat and the states stepping in

- **Timeline.**
  - 1 Feb 2025: Director Chopra fired.
  - 3 Feb: Acting Director Bessent ordered all work halted.
  - 7 Feb: Russell Vought became acting director.
  - 14 Feb: a federal judge barred data destruction and mass firing.
  - Source: [Wikipedia, CFPB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau) [F] (secondary; it cites NPR and NYT).
  - In April the bureau sent reduction-in-force notices to more than 1,400 staff. Chief legal officer Mark Paoletta argued that "an approximately 200 person agency" could meet the bureau's statutory duties ([OPB/NPR](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/15/cfpb-staff-layoffs-can-proceed-appeals-court-rules/) [F]).
  - On 15 August a 2-1 D.C. Circuit panel (Katsas; Pillard dissenting) vacated Judge Jackson's injunction against the layoffs.
  - In December the full court granted rehearing en banc and reinstated the stay ([AccountsRecovery](https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2025/12/18/full-d-c-circuit-to-rehear-cfpb-layoff-case-blocking-trump-administration-firings/) [S]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Enforcement dropped.**
  - The Zelle case against Early Warning Services, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo had alleged more than $870m of customer losses over seven years. It was dismissed with prejudice on 4 March ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/cfpb-drops-zelle-lawsuit-in-latest-reversal-under-trump-administration-204639032.html) [F]; [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/03/237009-cfpb-drops-zelle-fraud-lawsuit-against-major-us-banking-institutions/) [S]).
  - Cases against Capital One, Rocket, SoLo Funds and Navy Federal were also dropped ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/15/cfpb-staff-layoffs-can-proceed-appeals-court-rules/) [F]).
  - The data-broker rule was cancelled in May ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Congressional Review Act repeals (9 May).**
  - S.J.Res.18 killed the $5 overdraft cap for banks with more than $10bn of assets.
  - S.J.Res.28 killed the rule that would have let the CFPB supervise payment apps handling at least 50m transactions a year. The House votes were 217-211 and 219-211.
  - A CRA repeal bars "substantially similar" rules. Holland & Knight noted that the CFPB keeps its UDAAP (unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices) authority ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/congress-rolls-back-major-cfpb-rules.html) [S]; [H&K](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/cfpb-overdraft-and-digital-payment-rules-repealed) [S]).
  - VERIFIED: two independent law-firm summaries agree on the dates and resolution numbers.
- **Funding cut.**
  - The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed 4 July) cut the CFPB's cap from 12% to 6.5% of Fed operating expenses. That takes the FY2025 maximum from $823m to about $446m (-46%), the lowest share in the agency's history ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/09/trump-big-beautiful-bill-slashes-cfpb-funding-what-it-means.html) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **The states filled the gap.**
  - On 13 August the New York Attorney General sued Early Warning Services. The suit alleges more than $1bn of Zelle scam losses in 2017–2023 and says safeguards designed in 2019 were never adopted ([NY AG](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-sues-company-behind-zelle-enabling-widespread-fraud) [F]).
  - VERIFIED. INTERPRETATION: consumer-protection risk for payment firms has not gone away. It has fragmented to state AGs, which is harder to predict and more expensive to comply with.
- **Open banking (Section 1033) was reopened.**
  - The CFPB first moved to withdraw the 2024 rule and then, in July, chose "accelerated rulemaking".
  - An Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPR, August) asks 36 questions, including whether to keep the ban on data-access fees. A notice on 22 August stayed compliance dates.
  - In September JPMorgan and Plaid renewed their data-access deal "with a pricing structure" whose terms were not disclosed ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13117.html) [F]; [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46602/plaid-to-pay-jpmorgan-for-access-to-customer-data) [S]).
  - VERIFIED. The widely cited "$300m a year" fee figure for Plaid is unverified and has been disputed.
- **Synapse aftermath.**
  - On 21 August the CFPB sued the bankrupt middleware firm. A stipulated $1 penalty was entered on 12 September, which unlocks the Civil Penalty Fund for victims. The partner banks held $60–90m less than Synapse's records showed ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/synapse-financial-technologies-inc/) [F]; [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/cfpb-sues-synapse-plans-use-victims-fund-pay-end-users/758776/) [F]).
  - The Chapter 11 case was dismissed in November. The trustee had found up to $95m missing, and suits between Evolve, Lineage, AMG, American Bank and Yotta continue ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/synapse-bankruptcy-dismissed-cfpb-may-start-paying-victims) [F]).
  - About $46.2m was reportedly allocated on 28 November ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/12/256754-cfpb-allocates-46m-to-victims-of-synapse-fintech-collapse/) [S]). That is REPORTED, and it is below the estimated shortfall.
  - The FDIC's post-Synapse custodial-account recordkeeping proposal (daily reconciliation per owner) was opposed by BPI and the ABA in January 2025 ([FDIC comment file](https://fdic.gov/federal-register-publications/bank-policy-institute-2-other-organizations-rin-3064-ag07) [S]). I found no final rule in 2025.
  - The FDIC withdrew its 2024 brokered-deposit proposal on 3 March ([Seward & Kissel](https://www.sewkis.com/insights/fdic-withdraws-pending-proposed-rule-on-brokered-deposits/) [S]).
  - So the banking-as-a-service (BaaS) rule-tightening launched after Synapse was mostly abandoned. VERIFIED for the withdrawal. The status of the custodial rule is REPORTED.

### 2.2 US: prudential deregulation, debanking and M&A

- **Reputational risk.**
  - The OCC issued Bulletin 2025-4 on 20 March: examiners "should no longer examine for reputation risk" ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-4.html) [F]).
  - The Fed did the same on 23 June ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20250623a.htm) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Debanking executive order (EO 14331, 7 August).**
  - Regulators must remove reputational risk from guidance within 180 days and review banks within 120 days for "politicized or unlawful debanking", with fines or consent decrees possible.
  - The order cites "Operation Chokepoint" and the flagging of P2P payments containing "Trump" or "MAGA" ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/08/guaranteeing-fair-banking-for-all-americans/) [F]).
  - Covington notes that no statute outlaws all of the conduct the order labels unlawful ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/08/executive-order-raises-temperature-on-debanking-issues) [S]).
  - On 10 December the OCC reported preliminary findings. Between 2020 and 2023 all nine of the largest national banks restricted lawful sectors: oil and gas, coal, firearms, private prisons, tobacco, adult entertainment and digital assets ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-123.html) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Crypto access normalised.**
  - FDIC FIL-7-2025 (28 March) removed the prior-notice requirement for crypto activities ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2025/fdic-clarifies-process-banks-engage-crypto-related-activities) [F]).
  - On 15 August the Fed ended its novel-activities supervision program and rescinded SR 23-7 ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20250815a.htm) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Capital.**
  - The eSLR final rule (25 November; effective 1 April 2026) cuts holding-company tier 1 capital by about $13bn (<2%). The bank-subsidiary buffer becomes 50% of the method-1 GSIB surcharge, capped at 1% ([FDIC Hill statement](https://www.fdic.gov/news/speeches/2025/final-rule-modify-enhanced-supplementary-leverage-ratio) [F]; [OCC Bulletin 2025-41](https://occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-41.html) [S]; the Fed vote was 5-2).
  - Stress test: all 22 banks passed. Aggregate CET1 fell 1.8pp, from 13.4% to 11.6%, with about $550bn of losses absorbed. A proposal to average two years of results (April) would have produced a 2.3pp decline ([Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-june-dodd-frank-act-stress-test-introduction.htm) [F]).
  - No Basel III endgame re-proposal was issued in 2025. Bowman pointed to "early 2026" ([Reuters/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/feds-bowman-says-regulators-unveil-153855386.html) [S]).
  - VERIFIED, except that the Basel timing is REPORTED.
- **Failures.**
  - Pulaski Savings Bank, Chicago: closed 17 January, $49.5m of assets.
  - The Santa Anna National Bank, Texas: closed 27 June, $63.8m of assets, suspected fraud ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/resolutions/bank-failures/in-brief/2025) [F]; [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/occ-shutters-small-texas-bank-due-to-suspected-fraud) [S]).
  - VERIFIED. These were the only two failures in 2025.
- **M&A.**
  - Capital One completed its Discover acquisition on 18 May. It took on the Discover, PULSE and Diners networks and a $265bn community benefits plan ([SEC 8-K Ex.99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927628/000119312525122059/d934475dex991.htm) [F]). It began moving its debit cards onto the Discover network ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927628/000092762825000276/cof-20250930.htm) [S]).
  - Fifth Third–Comerica: $10.9bn all-stock, announced 6 October, creating about $288bn of assets ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/ria-news/fifth-third-to-acquire-comerica-in-109-billion-all-stock-deal/262405) [S]).
  - Huntington–Cadence: about $7.4bn, announced 27 October ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/acquisitions/2025/huntington-acquires-cadence-bank-for-7-4-billion/) [S]).
  - PNC–FirstBank: $4.1bn ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/the-five-biggest-bank-m-a-deals-of-2025) [S]).
  - By early November about 150 bank mergers worth roughly $45bn had closed. Average time to close was about 4 months, the shortest since at least 1990, against roughly 7 months at the Biden-era peak ([PYMNTS/FT](https://www.pymnts.com/bank-regulation/2025/bank-merger-approval-rates-hit-35-year-high/) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Private-credit and fraud stress.**
  - Tricolor, a subprime auto lender, filed to liquidate on 10 September amid a federal fraud probe. JPMorgan took a $170m charge-off. Dimon: "When you see one cockroach, there are probably more."
  - Fifth Third flagged an impairment of up to $200m. Jefferies' fund had $715m of exposure to First Brands ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/jpmorgan-dimon-tricolor-chargeoff-earnings/802740/) [F]).
  - First Brands left as much as $2.3bn of unpaid loans, with allegations that invoices were pledged more than once ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/jamie-dimon-warns-cockroaches-us-economy-credit-concerns-grow) [S]).
  - On 15–16 October Zions disclosed a $50m charge-off on loans to Cantor II and IV. Western Alliance disclosed its suit against Cantor Group V (about $100m, alleged forged title insurance). The regional bank index fell about 6% ([AML Intelligence](https://www.amlintelligence.com/2025/10/news-us-banking-stocks-hit-after-two-lenders-reveal-fraud-disclosures/) [F]; [InvestmentNews/Reuters](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/regional-bank-shares-tank-after-distressed-mortgage-loans-fraud-claims/262604) [S]).
  - VERIFIED. INTERPRETATION: the losses were small relative to capital. The real lesson is about verifying collateral and receivables, which is a data and identity problem, not a capital problem.

### 2.3 Stablecoins and crypto

- **GENIUS Act (P.L. 119-27).**
  - Signed 18 July.
  - Reserves: at least $1 of permitted reserves (cash, insured deposits, short T-bills, repo, government money-market funds) for every $1 of stablecoins.
  - Nonbank issuers below $10bn may opt into a state regime.
  - Issuers may not pay interest, but "holders" is undefined and exchanges are not restricted.
  - BSA/AML applies. Holders get priority in insolvency.
  - Effective date: the earlier of 18 January 2027 or 120 days after final rules ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-08-20_IN12553_0b329134f6be0cc12f78017358552f4053d52c5e.html) [F]; [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/07/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-signs-genius-act-into-law/) [F]; [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/banking-finance_1/united-states-the-genius-act-a-new-federal-framework-for-stablecoin-issuers-custodians-and-banks) [S]).
  - The administration's stated aim is Treasury demand and dollar dominance.
  - VERIFIED.
- **Circle IPO.**
  - 34m shares at $31, above the $27–28 range. About $1.05bn raised, roughly $6.9bn market cap. Opened at $69.50 and closed at $83.23 (+168%) on 5 June ([IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/the-ipo-buzz-circle-internet-group-prices-upsized-ipo-at-31-3-above-range/) [F]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **OCC charters (12 December).**
  - Conditional national trust charters: Circle ("First National Digital Currency Bank"), Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets and Paxos. They can take no deposits and make no loans. Coinbase, Bridge and Crypto.com were pending.
  - Comptroller Gould: new entrants "are good for consumers" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/382379/ripple-bitgo-and-paxos-secure-conditional-approval-for-us-banking-charters) [F]). Banks called it a "backdoor" ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/12/12/banks-crypto-occ-charters) [S]).
  - VERIFIED.
- **Other issuers.**
  - Klarna announced KlarnaUSD on the Tempo testnet in November ([Wikipedia/Klarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klarna) [F]; secondary). REPORTED.
- **Security incidents.**
  - Bybit: about $1.5bn stolen on 21 February. On 26 February the FBI attributed it to North Korea's "TraderTraitor" and listed 51 Ethereum addresses ([FBI IC3 PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250226) [F]).
  - Coinbase: bribed overseas support agents leaked data on 69,461 customers. The intrusion began 26 December 2024 and ran to 11 May 2025. The data included ID images and masked SSNs. Coinbase refused a $20m ransom and estimated the cost at $180–400m in an SEC filing ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/extortionists-bribed-coinbase-employees-to-give-them-customer-data-174713732.html) [F]; [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/coinbase-discloses-breach-faces-up-to-400-million-in-losses/) [S]).
  - VERIFIED. INTERPRETATION: both incidents were human or operational failures (signing-process compromise, insider bribery), not failures of the cryptography.

### 2.4 Fintech IPOs, valuations and enforcement

## 4. How things evolved (2023 → 2025 → 2026)

- **US consumer regulation swung sharply.**
  - 2024 brought the 1033 rule, the overdraft rule, the larger-participant rule, the Zelle suit and the post-Synapse proposals.
  - 2025 reversed nearly all of it.
  - In 2026, 1033 remained enjoined and its fee policy unresolved (per the CRS and secondary reports), and the CFPB staffing case was before the full D.C. Circuit.
- **Bank stress changed shape.** 2023 was a deposit run (SVB et al.). 2025 was credit fraud in nonbank lending (Tricolor, First Brands), with almost no bank failures.
- **Fraud kept climbing.** IC3 losses were $12.5bn (2023 data), $16.6bn (2024) and $20.9bn (2025). The UK held roughly flat at about £1.17bn after mandatory reimbursement. The EU rose from €3.5bn to €4.2bn and responded in 2025 by legislating liability (PSD3/PSR).
- **Stablecoins moved into the mainstream.** They went from enforcement-era hostility (2023) to statute (GENIUS, July 2025) and bank-style charters (December 2025). GENIUS rules were due by July 2026 and, per [S] sources, deadlines slipped.
- **Nigeria moved from crisis to consolidation.** After the 2023–24 FX crisis, the naira stabilised in 2025, the grey list was exited in October 2025, and recapitalisation was completed in March 2026.
- **Cross-border targets drifted.** The G20 2027 targets were officially judged "unlikely" in October 2025.

## 5. Implications for PayPal and the industry

- **Lighter federal oversight, but not less risk** (INTERPRETATION). Repealing the larger-participant rule removed routine CFPB supervision of PayPal and Venmo, but UDAAP authority remains. State AGs are now the active enforcers on P2P fraud: New York's suit against the Zelle operator is a template that could be applied to any P2P network.
- **Open-banking costs.** Bank data-access fees, starting with the JPMorgan–Plaid deal, raise the cost of account-linking and pay-by-bank funding that PayPal and Venmo rely on. JPMorgan reportedly set its highest fees for payments-focused firms ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13117.html) [F]). INTERPRETATION.
- **Stablecoin competition intensifies** (INTERPRETATION). GENIUS legitimises PYUSD but also lets banks, Circle (now chartered), Klarna and others compete. The yield ban on issuers, combined with no ban on exchanges paying yield, tilts the economics toward distribution platforms.
- **Agentic commerce is PayPal's main 2025 strategic bet.** It took a seat in AP2, adopted ACP with OpenAI and integrated Mastercard Agent Pay. The risk is that Visa and Mastercard tokenisation puts card networks at the centre of agent checkout. INTERPRETATION.
- **Europe is a liability and competition risk** (INTERPRETATION). PSD3/PSR impersonation refunds and payee-name checks will apply to PayPal's EU business. Wero launched first in Germany, PayPal's strongest European market, backed by the Sparkassen and Volksbanken. Those were the same bank groups that blocked PayPal debits in August 2025.
- **Operational resilience.** The German direct-debit incident shows that a fraud-control outage at one wallet can trigger systemic bank countermeasures. DORA-style third-party and incident rules now frame such events. INTERPRETATION.
- **Industry-wide.** Consolidation is accelerating (record-fast US approvals), redress costs are hitting UK lenders (motor finance), and AI is reshaping cost bases while the CBA and Klarna episodes show reputational limits on AI-driven job cuts.

## 6. Problems still unresolved as of November 2026 (evidence-backed)

- **The CFPB's future.** Staffing litigation went to en banc rehearing in December 2025 ([S]). The 1033 rule was enjoined in 2026, and data-access fees are unresolved ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13117.html) [F] plus [S] reports).
- **Synapse victims.** They have still not been made whole. The shortfall is up to $95m, and litigation among the banks is ongoing ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/synapse-bankruptcy-dismissed-cfpb-may-start-paying-victims) [F]).
- **Fraud is still growing.** US losses were $20.9bn in 2025 (+26%), and AI-enabled complaints were $893m ([IC3 2025](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf) [F]). In the EU, users bear about 85% of credit-transfer fraud ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2025/html/ecb.pr251215~e133d9d683.en.html) [F]).
- **EU rules not yet in force.** PSD3/PSR was still not formally adopted as of May 2026, and the digital euro still depends on 2026 legislation ([Wikipedia/PSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_Services_Directive) [F]; [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/html/index.en.html) [F]).
- **Cross-border costs.** Retail and remittance costs are far from the G20 targets. Sub-Saharan Africa is still the most expensive region because of USD cash dependence, weak competition and lack of non-bank access ([FSB](https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P091025-1.pdf) [F]).
- **Nigerian consumer harm.** Banking is the top complaint sector, loan-app pricing runs near 200% a year, and NIP usage is 69% male ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/09/11/fccpc-recovers-n10-billion-for-aggrieved-customers-following-complaints-against-banks-fintech-others/) [F]; [AfricaNenda](https://africanenda.org/wp-content/uploads/siips_2025_NIP-Nigeria_CaseStudy_en.pdf) [F]).
- **African cyber capacity.** Only 30% of African countries surveyed have an incident-reporting system ([INTERPOL](https://www.interpol.int/en/News-and-Events/News/2025/New-INTERPOL-report-warns-of-sharp-rise-in-cybercrime-in-Africa) [F]).
- **Collateral and receivables fraud in nonbank credit.** The 2025 cases show weak independent verification ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/jpmorgan-dimon-tricolor-chargeoff-earnings/802740/) [F]).
- **Ledger reconciliation in BaaS.** The FDIC custodial-recordkeeping proposal was never finalised in 2025 ([S]).

## 7. Opportunity signals (all SPECULATIVE)

1. **Payee verification and APP-scam reimbursement tooling for African instant-payment systems.** The UK and EU now mandate name checks and refunds (PSR, EP). Nigeria's NIP runs 11bn transactions a year and is moving to ISO 20022 (AfricaNenda). Products could include name-matching APIs, mule-account scoring, and reimbursement-claims workflows for banks and fintechs ahead of a CBN mandate.
2. **Cross-border corridors in local currency or stablecoins for Africa.** The FSB names USD-cash dependence and lack of non-bank access as the SSA cost drivers. COMESA targets costs below 3% against about 8% today. GENIUS gives USD stablecoins legal clarity. There is room for compliant settlement layers that link NIP, Kenya's PesaLink or M-Pesa, and Egypt's InstaPay, with on- and off-ramps.
3. **Agent-ready checkout for African merchants.** AP2, ACP, Visa Intelligent Commerce and Agent Pay are open or partner protocols (Google, Visa, PayPal). A connector layer could expose African catalogues and local rails (NIP, mobile money) to AI agents with signed mandates. That addresses a gap the global protocols are unlikely to prioritise.
4. **Post-grey-list compliance infrastructure.** Nigeria and South Africa exited the FATF list. Recapitalised banks raised ₦4.65tn, including 27% from foreign investors. Correspondent banks will still demand evidence. Shared KYC/KYB utilities and AML audit-trail products could serve them, alongside behavioural-AI monitoring, which was CBN's 2026 direction per TechCabal's headlines.
5. **Responsible digital credit.** FCCPC rate monitoring, about 198% APR examples, and banking and fintech as the top complaint sectors suggest demand for transparent, regulator-ready lending stacks: affordability checks, APR disclosure and complaint handling.
6. **Ledger-of-record and reconciliation for sponsor banks and microfinance banks (MFBs).** The Synapse lesson (a $60–95m gap) and the shelved FDIC rule mean independent per-customer reconciliation is a sellable trust product for BaaS-style partnerships in Nigeria and elsewhere.
7. **Women-first financial products.** Account ownership among Nigerian women is 52.2% against 63.3% overall, and men make 69% of NIP volume (Findex, AfricaNenda). There is room for women-targeted savings and payments with agent-network distribution. Agents rose 20% to 28m globally (GSMA).
8. **Deepfake and voice-clone defence for bank contact centres.** IC3 recorded $893m of AI-related losses, the FBI issued a voice-clone PSA, and the CBA reversal shows banks still need human-plus-AI support. Liveness detection and caller authentication would serve this need.

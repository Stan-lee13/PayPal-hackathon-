## 2. Findings by country

### India

- **2024: Paytm Payments Bank wound down.** RBI barred Paytm Payments Bank (PPBL) from accepting credits after 15 Mar 2024; the original deadline was 29 Feb. RBI then asked NPCI to move users of the "@paytm" UPI handle to 4–5 other banks to "minimise concentration risk in the UPI system". One97 shares hit a record low of Rs 318.05 on 16 Feb 2024, against a high of about Rs 998 in October 2023. One97 says it serves 333m consumers and 20m merchants. VERIFIED. [Business Standard [F]](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paytm-gains-5-as-rbi-asks-npci-to-become-third-party-application-provider-124022600122_1.html)
  - *Why:* persistent non-compliance, which I take from the RBI framing quoted in the press. I could not fetch the RBI press release itself: its page rendered only the site shell. Treat the cause as REPORTED.
- **2024: UPI market-share cap deferred again.** NPCI pushed the deadline to 31 Dec 2026. It had first been set in 2020 and was deferred in 2022.
  - In November 2024 UPI processed 15.48bn transactions: PhonePe 7.4bn (about 48%) and Google Pay 5.739bn (about 37%).
  - The enforcement plan sends warnings at 25–27% and 27.1–30% share, then bars an app from onboarding new users above 30%.
  - VERIFIED. [MediaNama [F]](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-npci-extends-upi-market-share-cap-deadline-to-2026-google-phonepe-relief/)
  - *Interpretation:* the 2024 Paytm episode showed how much concentration risk sits in third-party apps. Even so, NPCI chose not to disrupt the two dominant apps.
- **UPI scale.**
  - October 2025: 20.7bn transactions worth Rs 27.28 lakh crore (volume +25%, value +16% y/y). [Entrackr [F]](https://entrackr.com/news/upi-hits-all-time-high-of-207-bn-transactions-in-october-10612233)
  - September 2026: 24.07bn transactions worth Rs 29.37 lakh crore, averaging 802m a day. August 2026 was the monthly volume record at 24.51bn. [StartupTalky citing NPCI [F]](https://startuptalky.com/india-upi-transactions-september-2026/)
  - REPORTED. npci.org.in returned 403, so I could not check the primary data.
- **Cyber fraud, 2023–24.** These figures come from MHA/I4C, based on NCRP and CFCFRMS data:
  - Losses were Rs 22,845.73 crore in 2024, up from Rs 7,465.18 crore in 2023.
  - More than 22.6 lakh incidents were reported in 2024 (+42%).
  - The Suspect Registry, launched in September 2024, has shared 24 lakh "Layer 1" mule accounts and prevented Rs 4,631 crore of losses.
  - I4C found that 46% of frauds reported in January–April 2024 originated in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar.
  - MHA did not give a resolution status for 19.18 lakh complaints, saying that is a state matter.
  - VERIFIED. [Scroll [F]](https://scroll.in/latest/1084796/indians-lost-rs-22845-crore-to-cyber-fraud-in-2024-a-206-rise-from-previous-year-centre), [The Wire [F]](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/cyber-crime-cases-see-annual-increase-by-at-least-40-rs-30000-crore-lost-to-fraud-since-2023-govt)
- **Bank frauds, FY2024-25 (RBI Annual Report).**
  - Cases fell to 23,953 from 36,060, but the amount rose to Rs 36,014 crore from Rs 12,230 crore.
  - Most of the rise in value comes from re-reporting 122 old cases worth Rs 18,674 crore. That followed the Supreme Court's March 2023 natural-justice ruling.
  - Card and internet frauds fell to 13,516 cases (Rs 1,457 crore) from 29,082 (Rs 520 crore): fewer cases, but larger amounts.
  - Public-sector banks account for 71% of fraud value.
  - REPORTED (Deccan Chronicle quoting the Annual Report). [Deccan Chronicle [F]](https://deccanchronicle.com/nation/bank-fraud-amount-triples-to-36014-cr-in-fy25-despite-fewer-cases-rbi-1882116)
- **2025: Digital lending rules consolidated.** RBI's Digital Lending Directions 2025 (issued 8 May 2025) apply to all regulated entities and their lending service providers and apps. Key provisions:
  - Outsourcing does not move responsibility away from the lender.
  - Borrowers get a cooling-off period of at least one day.
  - Funds must flow directly between lender and borrower, not through the service provider's accounts.
  - REPORTED (law-firm summary). [IndiaLaw [F]](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/rbi-notifies-digital-lending-directions/)

## 4. How things changed over time

- **2023:** losses were left with victims. Australia's banks reimbursed 2–5% of scam losses, and GCash victims were blamed for sharing OTPs.
- **2024:** states intervened. Singapore's SRF took effect, India launched its Suspect Registry, Vietnam introduced biometrics, the Philippines passed AFASA and the Paytm bank was shut.
- **2025:** liability laws were written: Australia's SPF Act, the AFASA implementing rules, India's digital lending directions and Brazil's MED 2.0.
- **2026:** enforcement deadlines arrived (Australia's AFCA requirement 1 Sep 2026 and SPF obligations 31 Mar 2027; UPI cap 31 Dec 2026). Rates rose again, which adds credit-stress risk to the small banks China is merging away.

## 5. Unresolved problems as of Nov 2026 (evidence-backed)

1. **Scam victims still recover very little.** Malaysia has returned RM10.9m against RM5.3bn+ lost. Brazil's MED has recovered about 8% (R$1.4bn of R$17.6bn). Australia's banks reimbursed 2–7% before the SPF. India gave no resolution data for 19 lakh complaints.
2. **Mule accounts are the shared choke point.** India has flagged 24 lakh mule accounts. Vietnam saw a 72% drop after biometrics. A Sumsub survey says 1 in 3 Latin Americans have been approached to act as mules. Brazil's BCB names "pulverisation" across many accounts as the core problem.
3. **Infrastructure-vendor risk.** Brazil's C&M breach shows that the companies connecting small fintechs to central-bank systems are a single point of failure.
4. **Concentration.** UPI's 85% duopoly persists despite three deferrals of the cap.
5. **Open banking is late outside Brazil.** Chile slipped to July 2027 and Canada has no committed date.
6. **CBDC disappointment.** Brazil dropped Drex's blockchain pilot, and China had to add interest to e-CNY to drive use.
7. **Small-bank fragility in China,** where risk is being absorbed through mergers rather than resolved.

## 6. Opportunity signals (SPECULATIVE)

- **Mule-account detection as a shared service.** Products that score accounts on the receiving side and share signals across institutions. Evidence: India's Suspect Registry, MED 2.0's multi-layer tracing and Malaysia's NSRC freezes. Highly relevant to Nigeria's NIP rails.
- **Tools for complying with liability regimes.** As Singapore, Australia (from March 2027) and the Philippines shift scam losses to banks and telcos, they will buy real-time surveillance, case management and AFCA/EDR claims tooling. Australia's possible A$3,000 auto-compensation would create demand for automated claims decisioning.
- **Biometric step-up for high-value push payments.** Vietnam's results (−50% cases, −72% mule receivers) suggest a playbook other countries could copy, including countries with BVN/NIN identity schemes.
- **Security and monitoring for central-bank connectivity providers.** C&M-type vendors need insider-threat and anomaly controls.
- **Cross-border QR acceptance and settlement.** QRIS–China flows are heavily one-way and Alipay-only. Japan's QR link is new. Merchant-side FX and settlement services could fill the gap.
- **Wallet-to-bank licensing support.** Mercado Pago in Argentina and Mexico shows the limits of operating as a payment provider only.
- **For PayPal:** Paidy faces rising Japanese rates and a market where cards hold 83% of cashless value. Open-banking delays in Canada and Chile slow pay-by-bank. Rising scam liability in APAC raises fraud-cost expectations for wallets.

## 2. Detailed findings by theme

### 2.1 The 2023 US banking crisis: causes re-examined (2023 → 2026)

- **Mark-to-market view, 2023.** Jiang, Matvos, Piskorski and Seru marked bank assets to market for the Q1 2022 to Q1 2023 rate rise. They found an average decline of 10%, about $2 trillion in aggregate. Their model shows that uninsured leverage can trigger *self-fulfilling solvency runs even when assets are liquid*, and that "many U.S. banks would have been at risk" without intervention. The paper was later published in the JFE in 2024 ([NBER w31048][F](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31048)).
- **CRE extension, 2024.** The same authors put CRE at about 25% of average bank assets ($2.7tn). 14% of all CRE loans and 44% of office loans are in negative equity. A 10% or 20% CRE default rate would mean $80bn or $160bn of losses. After the 2022 rate shock, 217 to 441 more banks ($0.3tn to $0.9tn in assets) would be underwater on a mark-to-market basis. Dozens to more than 300 smaller regional banks could face solvency runs ([Minneapolis Fed conference paper, Sept 2024][F](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/-/media/assets/events/2024/macroeconomic-policy-perspectives-banking-regulation-and-macroeconomic-outcomes/monetary-tightening-commercial-real-estate-distress-and-us-bank-fragility-0924.pdf)).
- **Business-model view, 2025.** Kelly and Rose of the Chicago Fed offer "seven facts that depart from the standard account." They describe the crisis as a reaction to banks serving crypto-asset firms and venture capital, two sectors under pressure in 2022. In their view this explains *which* banks ran better than balance-sheet metrics do, and supervisors should watch business models and institutional depositors ([Chicago Fed WP 2025-04][F](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2025/wp2025-04.pdf)).
- **Speed of runs, 2023.** In Cookson, Fox, Gil-Bazo, Imbet and Schiller, banks in the top tercile of pre-run Twitter exposure lost 6.6pp more stock value. Tweet volume in the prior four hours predicted hourly losses, and social media was associated with larger uninsured deposit outflows in Q1 2023 ([Dallas Fed/CEMLA conference paper][F](https://www.dallasfed.org/-/media/documents/research/events/2023/23cemla/23cemla_cooksonpaper.pdf)).
- **Supervisory lessons, 2024.** Sarin and Schuermann report that SVB and Signature had uninsured deposit shares of 94% and 89%. They recommend four changes: multiple stress scenarios, disclosure of stressed fair value for *all* securities, funding and liquidity stress tests, and wider bank coverage ([Boston Fed conference paper][F](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/events/2024/stress-testing-research-conference/Sarin_Stress_Testing_Lessons_from_the_Banking_Turmoil_of_2023.pdf)).
- **Root cause, 2026.** Mertens, Paul and Schneider show that maturity mismatch has more than tripled since the mid-1980s, in lockstep with falling rates and term premia. Banks "reach for duration" to cover operating costs. This is the slow-moving source of 2023-style losses ([SF Fed WP 2026-16][F](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/wp2026-16.pdf)).
- **Policy responses outside the US.** The UK raised FSCS deposit protection from £85,000 to £120,000 on 1 Dec 2025. Temporary high balances are now covered up to £1.4m for six months ([FSCS][F](https://www.fscs.org.uk/industry-resources/deposit-limit-change-stakeholder-materials/)).

*INTERPRETATION:* The academic record has moved from "rates plus uninsured deposits" toward "rates plus uninsured deposits plus concentrated depositor networks plus social-media coordination." A deposit franchise built on one tight-knit sector, such as tech startups or crypto firms, is now treated as a run-risk factor in its own right.

### 2.2 Fraud, scams and the liability gap (2023 → 2026)

- **US P2P scams, 2024 testimony.** Consumer Reports told the Senate PSI in May 2024 that 64% of Americans use P2P apps. Zelle "does not have a public-facing policy" on reimbursement, and banks deny scam claims as "authorized." FTC 2023 data put losses via payment apps and services at $1.86bn, ahead of crypto at $1.41bn. The four largest banks reported $255m in Zelle-related disputes for 2021 to H1 2022 ([CR testimony PDF][F](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Fraud-Alert_-Shedding-Light-on-Zelle.Delicia-Hand.May-2024.pdf)). The PSI staff report itself (July 2024) was blocked: hsgac.senate.gov PDFs returned 403. Search summaries say three banks rejected about $880m of disputed Zelle claims in 2021–2023, and that reimbursement of unauthorized-fraud claims fell from 62% (2019) to 38% (2023) [S]. These figures were not independently fetched here.
- **Fed infrastructure is voluntary and incomplete.** The Federal Reserve Banks published an operational scam definition (2023), the ScamClassifier model (2024) and a Scams Mitigation Toolkit (2025). A 2024 industry group *recommended* an independent cross-rail scam information exchange and admitted it "will take time" ([FedPayments Improvement][F](https://fedpaymentsimprovement.org/strategic-initiatives/payments-security/scams/)).
- **Public support for legislation.** An ABA-commissioned poll found 92% of Americans support the SCAM Act ([ABA][F](https://www.aba.com/about-us/press-room/press-releases/consumer-survey-banking-methods-2024)). That page is the 2024 channel survey; the SCAM Act poll appears as a linked item, and I did not open the poll itself.
- **Industrialised scams, 2026.** An AI voice-agent honeypot captured 10,211 scam and spam calls (913 hours) from 5,780 numbers. Scams keep office hours, with 6.6 times more calls per weekday than per weekend day. Thirty recycled script clusters carry most of the traffic, and the top five account for half. Callers seek identity anchors such as address and date of birth more than payment credentials ([arXiv 2608.24127][F](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24127)).
- **AI fraud vectors (2025–26).** FINRA's 2026 oversight report (9 Dec 2025) lists account takeover, new-account fraud using stolen or synthetic identities bought on the dark web, GenAI imposter sites, fake IDs, deepfake audio and video, polymorphic malware and cybercrime-as-a-service. GenAI gets its own new section ([FINRA][F](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/cybersecurity)). Deloitte (May 2024) projects US GenAI-enabled fraud losses of $40bn by 2027 from $12.3bn in 2023, a 32% CAGR in its aggressive scenario. It also cites a 700% rise in fintech deepfake incidents in 2023 ([Deloitte][F](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/financial-services/deepfake-banking-fraud-risk-on-the-rise.html)). This is a projection.
- **Europe: SCA works for cards; consumers still fear refunds.** France recorded €1.195bn of payment fraud in 2023 across 7.1m fraudulent operations. The card fraud rate of 0.053% is the lowest on record, and the online card fraud rate is down 35% since 2019 ([Banque de France/OSMP][F](https://www.banque-france.fr/fr/actualites/fraude-aux-moyens-de-paiement-stabilisation-des-taux-de-fraude-un-niveau-historiquement-bas-mais-la)). A BEUC survey of 10,227 people in 10 eurozone countries (May–June 2025) found that 22% of adults had been victims of digital-payment fraud or a breach in the past five years. 86% worry about not getting their money back, and 55% of digital-payment users report difficulties ([BEUC][F](https://www.beuc.eu/press-release/consumers-expect-digital-euro-be-safe-and-reliable-easy-use-and-free)).
- **Public-sector fraud in the UK.** The NAO estimates that fraud and error cost the UK taxpayer £55bn–£81bn in 2023-24. It sees a "clear mismatch" between the scale of the problem and plans for data analytics ([NAO, July 2025][F](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/using-data-analytics-to-tackle-fraud-and-error/)).
- **Africa signal.** In Ghana, mobile money processed GHC518.4bn in December 2025 across more than 26.7m active accounts. SMS and USSD impersonation scams are growing, and the research corpus has only 220 labelled messages, which shows how scarce training data is ([arXiv 2610.40544][F](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40544)).

### 2.3 Cyber and operational resilience (2023 → 2026)

- **IMF GFSR, April 2024, chapter 3.** Almost one-fifth of reported cyber incidents over two decades hit financial firms, and banks are the most frequent target. Direct losses total about $12bn since 2004 and $2.5bn since 2020. The median loss is about $0.4m and three-quarters are below $2.8m, but the probability of an extreme loss of $2.5bn or more has risen. Banks have the lowest median cyber ratings among financial firms, and emerging and developing economies lag on legislation and strategy ([IMF eLibrary][F](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9798400257704/CH003.xml)).
- **Ransomware in financial services, 2024.** 65% of financial-services firms were hit, against 64% in 2023, 55% in 2022 and 34% in 2021. 62% restored data from backups and 51% paid the ransom. When backups were compromised, the median recovery cost was $3m against $375k, and the median initial demand was $2.24m against $1m ([Sophos 2024][F](https://assets.sophos.com/X24WTUEQ/at/wn2jvpknb6n7w6xs343q536g/sophos-state-of-ransomware-financial-services-2024.pdf)). This is vendor survey data.
- **ECB cyber stress test, July 2024.** The test covered 109 banks under a scenario in which "all preventive measures failed" and core databases were hit. 28 banks ran actual IT recovery tests. Frameworks exist, but "areas for improvement remain." Results fed into the 2024 SREP, and the ECB reports a surge in cyber incidents ([ECB Banking Supervision][F](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ssm.pr240726~06d5776a02.en.html)).
- **UK bank IT outages (2023–25).** The Treasury Committee's pages are blocked (403). Search summaries say the nine largest banks and building societies reported at least 158 IT failures and 803 hours of unplanned outages between January 2023 and February 2025. In Barclays' January 2025 outage, 56% of online payments failed, with an expected £5m–£7.5m in compensation [S]. Not independently fetched here.

### 2.4 Crypto, stablecoins and AML (2023 → 2026)

- **2023 enforcement wave.** The CFTC brought 96 actions in FY2023, producing more than $4.3bn in penalties, restitution and disgorgement. 47 of them (over 49%) involved digital assets, including FTX (more than $8bn of customer assets lost) and Binance (illegal derivatives exchange and willful evasion) ([CFTC][F](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8822-23)).
- **Demand-side scepticism, Feb 2024.** 63% of US adults have little or no confidence that crypto is safe and reliable. Only 17% have ever used it, a share unchanged since 2021 ([Pew][F](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/10/24/majority-of-americans-arent-confident-in-the-safety-and-reliability-of-cryptocurrency/)). In Canada, crypto use for payments among 18–34s rose from 4% (2024) to 10% (2025) ([Payments Canada][F](https://www.payments.ca/canadian-payment-transactions-total-more-12-trillion-2025-real-time-payments-appeal-over-half)).
- **GENIUS Act design, July 2025.** It requires 100% HQLA reserves, monthly attestations and BSA obligations, and bans issuers from paying interest. Payment stablecoins are neither securities nor commodities. Tokenized deposits are preserved and may pay interest ([Richmond Fed, 18 Nov 2025][F](https://www.richmondfed.org/banking/banker_resources/news_flash/2025/20251118_genius_act)).
- **Implementation fights, March 2026.** Brookings puts USD stablecoins at about $280bn at end-2025, up from about $25bn in 2020, and notes Bessent's forecast of $3tn by 2030. Its key issue is that GENIUS does not stop affiliates or third parties from paying "rewards." Brookings names Coinbase's USDC rewards and **PayPal's PYUSD rewards** as examples and notes that banks fear deposit flight. It recommends enforcing interest restrictions at first. It also reports that the Fed is weighing capped, non-interest "skinny" master accounts for chartered stablecoin issuers ([Brookings][F](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/next-steps-for-genius-payment-stablecoins/)).
- **Market-structure stalemate, Sept 2026.** The Senate "failed to invoke cloture on the CLARITY Act" on 15 Sept 2026. House chairs said they would work with regulators "utiliz[ing] existing authorities" until a statute passes ([House FSC][F](https://financialservices.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=411233)). This fits the earlier slices' 49–50 vote count.
- **Political money, Nov 2026.** Americans for Financial Reform counts more than $60m of crypto-backed PAC spending in Ohio's Senate race. It also welcomed Opportunity Financial dropping its bid to buy a bank and called for scrutiny of BNPL and crypto firms seeking charters ([AFR][F](https://ourfinancialsecurity.org/news/)).
- **Illicit-finance scrutiny, 2026.** The Senate PSI library lists "Crypto and Iran's Shadow Banking Network" (28 Sept 2026) and a letter from Ranking Member Blumenthal to Tether (22 Oct 2026). Only the titles could be read; the documents returned 403 ([HSGAC PSI library][F](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/subcommittees/investigations/library/)).
- **AML failures by machine.** The Central Bank of Ireland fined Coinbase Europe €21,464,734 (6 Nov 2025). Configuration faults left more than 30m transactions, worth over €176bn and about 31% of activity, improperly monitored for 12 months. Remediation took about three years and produced 2,708 late STRs ([CBI][F](https://www.centralbank.ie/news-media/press-releases/press-release-enforcement-action-against-coinbase-europe-limited-6-November-2025)). Cato argues that FinCEN received more than 28.7m reports in FY2025 against roughly 275 investigations ([Cato][S]).

### 2.5 Payments structure: rails, cash and fees (2023 → 2026)

- **US legacy rails still dominate.** ACH handled 35.2bn payments in 2025 (+4.9%) worth $93tn (+7.9%). Same Day ACH reached 1.4bn payments and $3.9tn (+16.7% in volume), and December 2025 set a monthly record of 3.22bn ([Nacha][F](https://www.nacha.org/content/ach-network-volume-and-value-statistics)). Consumers made 47 payments a month on average in 2025, and the share using cash fell from 83% to 81%, while check use fell from 35% to 33% ([Atlanta Fed SDCPC][F](https://www.atlantafed.org/banking-and-payments/consumer-payments/survey-and-diary-of-consumer-payment-choice)). 55% of bank customers use the mobile app as their main channel, with branches at 8% ([ABA 2024][F](https://www.aba.com/about-us/press-room/press-releases/consumer-survey-banking-methods-2024)).
- **Canada is late on real-time.** Canada recorded 26.7bn retail transactions worth C$12.9tn in 2025. Credit cards account for 43% of volume and debit for 25%; cash is 9%. The Real-Time Rail launches in Q4 2026, and 52% of Canadians find real-time payments appealing ([Payments Canada][F](https://www.payments.ca/canadian-payment-transactions-total-more-12-trillion-2025-real-time-payments-appeal-over-half)). The Bank of Canada now supervises PSPs under the RPAA and has "new mandates for stablecoins and consumer-driven banking" ([BoC][F](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/retail-payments-supervision/)).
- **European A2A at scale.** Spain's bank-owned Bizum reports 31.5m active users and 5.5bn cumulative transactions, and has added in-store NFC payments ([Bizum][F](https://bizum.es/en/)).
- **Cash decline and access.** In Germany (2023), cash fell to 51% of transactions. Debit cards had 27% and mobile 6%, a threefold rise. By value, debit is 32% and cash 26%. Cards are accepted at 80% of points of sale, and 15% of people say ATM or branch access is difficult, up from 6% ([Bundesbank][F](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/press/press-releases/payment-behaviour-in-germany-in-2023-934894)). In Sweden only 10% paid cash for their last in-store purchase, against about half of in-store purchases in the euro area (67% in Malta, 22% in the Netherlands). The Riksbank recommends SEK 1,000 of cash per adult and a SEK 10,000 retail cash-purchase limit ([Riksbank Payments Report 2025][F](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/payments--cash/payments-in-sweden/payments-report-2025/trends-on-the-payments-market/the-swedish-payments-market-is-almost-entirely-digital/cash-use-continues-to-decline/)). In the UK, the Treasury commissioned an Access to Banking review (report due Oct 2026). Lloyds is closing hundreds of branches, and 237 banking hubs are open with 39 planned ([MoneySavingExpert][F](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/news/2026/05/bank-branch-access-review/)).
- **Fees and surcharging.** On 31 March 2026 Australia decided to remove surcharging on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa, lower interchange caps and add fee transparency, effective 1 Oct 2026. The foreign-card interchange cap follows on 1 Apr 2027. It will consult on regulating mobile wallets, three-party networks and BNPL ([RBA][F](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-10.html)).
- **B2B gap.** Capgemini's World Payments Report 2027 (24 Sept 2026) finds that 60% of banks prioritised B2B payment innovation, yet only 32% of corporates are satisfied with their main bank. Only 21% of banks are scaling an "accelerated intelligent money" instrument such as stablecoins or tokenized deposits ([Capgemini][F](https://www.capgemini.com/insights/research-library/world-payments-report/)).

### 2.6 Consumers, credit and access (2023 → 2026)

- **Unbanked churn.** Citing the FDIC 2023 survey, Brookings' Aaron Klein (Senate Banking, Feb 2025) said 5.6m households (4.2%) were unbanked. Just under 6% more had been unbanked at some point in the past year, so about 10% churn in and out. The top reason is failing to meet minimum balances, and overdraft fees are a key unpredictable cost ([Brookings testimony][F](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Debanking-Testimony-of-Aaron-Klein-Final-post-review-ATK-clean.pdf)). - **Credit risk is drifting up, carefully.** In Q2 2026, large-bank card balances rose 3.4% year on year to a record. The share of new accounts going to sub-660 scores rose from 16.1% to 18.5%, while the average new line fell from $6,216 to $6,066. Delinquencies and charge-offs fell, and mortgage 90+ days-past-due was 0.86% ([Philadelphia Fed][F](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/2026-q2-large-bank)).
- **The CFPB in court.** CourtListener lists a published D.C. Circuit opinion, *NTEU v. ## 3. Contradictions and disputed claims

1. **Why the 2023 runs happened.** Balance-sheet models (NBER; Minneapolis Fed paper) predict fragility from uninsured leverage and mark-to-market losses. The Chicago Fed's Kelly and Rose argue those metrics flag banks that did *not* run and miss some that did, and point to depositor-sector concentration instead. Cookson et al. add social-media coordination. These views partly complement each other, but they lead to different policy: capital and liquidity rules versus surveillance of business models and depositor networks. DISPUTED.
2. **Stablecoin supply at end-2025.** Brookings says "nearly $280bn." The earlier y-slices used DefiLlama at about $304bn. The gap probably reflects which coins count as "USD stablecoins" and the data vendor. DISPUTED (definitional). Cite the source together with its definition.
3. **What drives debanking.** Brookings frames it as economics: low balances, overdraft costs and AML compliance costs. Cato [S] frames it as government pressure through reputational-risk rules and BSA confidentiality. The House "Operation Choke Point 2.0" framing, seen only in snippets, sides with Cato on crypto. The evidence favours both, for different populations: low-income consumers versus crypto and politically exposed firms. INTERPRETATION.
4. **AI fraud scale.** Deloitte's $40bn by 2027 is an upper-scenario projection. Observed data, such as earlier slices' IC3 and FTC totals, measure all fraud and do not isolate GenAI's share. No source fetched here measures realised GenAI-attributable losses. Treat the figure as SPECULATIVE until measured.
5. **Cyber loss magnitude.** The IMF says median direct losses are small (about $0.4m), while Sophos reports median ransomware recovery costs of $375k to $3m. The two are consistent, but headlines such as "cyber costs banks billions" mostly come from indirect losses and tail events, not typical incidents.

Brookings flags stablecoin "rewards," including PayPal PYUSD. PSI turns to Tether and Iran-linked crypto. Researchers show scams are industrialised, running office hours on recycled scripts.

## 5. Implications for PayPal and the industry (INTERPRETATION)

- **PYUSD rewards are now a named policy target.** Brookings cites PayPal's PYUSD rewards as an example of the affiliate-reward loophole that banks want closed. If regulators or a revived CLARITY bill close it, PYUSD's main growth lever, rewards-driven supply, weakens.
- **The surcharging and fee regime is moving toward wallets.** Australia is explicitly next consulting on mobile wallets and three-party networks; PayPal operates both. Card fee caps also shift merchant economics toward card acceptance, which narrows the price gap PayPal relies on.
- **Scam liability is a reputational and regulatory exposure for all P2P apps.** US consumers' main complaint is the "authorized" denial. PayPal and Venmo face the same expectations that Consumer Reports set out for Zelle.
- **Real-time A2A rails keep scaling:** Bizum's 31.5m users, Canada's RTR and Nacha Same Day ACH +17%. Wallet differentiation must rest on trust, protection and cross-border reach rather than speed.

## 7. Opportunity signals (SPECULATIVE, each tied to evidence)

1. **Scam-intelligence exchange as a service.** A neutral, cross-rail consortium that shares scam signals (mule accounts, script fingerprints, phone numbers) among banks, wallets and telcos. Evidence: the Fed group recommended such an exchange but none exists ([FedPI][F]), and scam operations reuse 30 script clusters from disposable numbers ([arXiv][F]). In Nigeria the same model could sit on NIBSS and NIP plus telco data.
2. **Authorized-fraud insurance or guarantees for P2P and merchants.** Pricing and underwriting scam-reimbursement cover for fintechs and banks, especially if US policy moves toward UK-style reimbursement. Evidence: the consumer refund fears in BEUC's 86% figure and the Zelle reimbursement gap ([BEUC][F]; [CR][F]).
3. **AI voice and SMS honeypots plus classifiers for emerging markets.** Labelled scam corpora are tiny (220 messages in Ghana) while MoMo volumes are huge (GHC518bn a month). A data-network business that collects scam messages in local languages and licenses detection models to MNOs and banks. Evidence: [arXiv Ghana][F] and [arXiv honeypot][F].
4. **Configuration assurance for AML systems.** Continuous testing that transaction-monitoring scenarios actually fire, a kind of "unit tests for AML rules." Evidence: Coinbase Europe's coding faults left €176bn unmonitored ([CBI][F]).
5. **Cyber-recovery-as-a-service for mid-size banks and fintechs.** Isolated immutable backups and tested recovery runbooks. Evidence: compromised backups raised median recovery costs from $375k to $3m ([Sophos][F]), and the ECB found recovery gaps ([ECB][F]).
6. **Bank-run early-warning analytics.** Monitoring social-media sentiment together with depositor concentration for treasurers and supervisors ([Dallas Fed paper][F]; [Chicago Fed][F]).
7. **Tokenized-deposit and stablecoin B2B treasury tooling.** Only 21% of banks are scaling such instruments, and corporate satisfaction is 32% ([Capgemini][F]). GENIUS lets tokenized deposits pay interest ([Richmond Fed][F]).
8. **Shared cash and branch utilities.** Banking hubs and cash-in-transit regulation show that shared infrastructure for declining cash is a regulated growth niche. Evidence: 237 UK hubs ([MSE][F]), and Australia designated Armaguard under its Cash Distribution Framework Act 2026 ([RBA mr-26-25][F](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-25.html)).

# Customer harm & customer experience in banking and payments, 2023 to Nov 2026 (slice: "customers")

Scope: complaints, account restrictions and frozen funds, debanking and account closures, disputes and chargebacks, failed transactions and refunds, fees, outages, customer-service automation, and consumer-protection enforcement. Regions: US, UK, EU, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Ghana, India and Brazil. Evidence tags: **[F]** means I opened and read the page or document. **[S]** means I only saw a search snippet. Claim labels: VERIFIED, REPORTED, DISPUTED, INTERPRETATION, SPECULATIVE. Event dates and publication dates are given separately where they differ.

Method note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, all sources came from fetching primary sites and APIs directly. The **CFPB Consumer Complaint Database API**, the **CBN document API** and the **Banco Central do Brasil complaint-ranking API** gave company-level and half-yearly data that search snippets would not have provided.

## 1. Key findings (top 10)

1. **US complaints to the CFPB roughly doubled every year**: about 1.66m in 2023, 3.19m in 2024 and 6.64m in 2025. About 88% of 2025 complaints concerned credit reporting. The CFPB itself flags misuse of the complaint channel by credit-repair firms. VERIFIED. [CFPB 2025 report, F](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2025-cr-annual-report_2026-03.pdf)
2. **Payments complaints jumped in 2025**. Money-services complaints went from about 27,400 to about 102,900. Domestic money-transfer complaints rose 1,317% against the prior two-year average, and the CFPB links the spike to the enforcement announcements of early 2025. In the public database, 2025 was dominated by Block/Cash App (42,569) and Zelle operator Early Warning (19,703). VERIFIED. [CFPB 2025 report, F](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2025-cr-annual-report_2026-03.pdf); [CFPB complaint DB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/search/api/v1/?size=0&product=Money%20transfer%2C%20virtual%20currency%2C%20or%20money%20service)
3. **PayPal's published CFPB complaints nearly tripled**: 3,834 in 2023, 4,143 in 2024, 7,412 in 2025 and 10,989 in 2026 up to 3 November. Monthly volume rose from roughly 400–500 in early 2025 to roughly 1,000–1,270 in 2026. The top 2026 issues are "unauthorized transactions" and "trouble accessing funds". VERIFIED (data). The causes are an INTERPRETATION. [CFPB DB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/search/api/v1/?size=0&company=Paypal%20Holdings%2C%20Inc)
4. **Federal consumer enforcement in the US was largely rolled back in 2025.** The CFPB dropped its Zelle suit on 4 March 2025. It terminated the Navy Federal ($80m overdraft refunds) and Apple Card orders. Congress voided the $5 overdraft rule on 9 May 2025. **The states stepped in**: New York sued Zelle's operator, and 46 attorneys general got a $45m Cash App settlement that locks in the CFPB redress of $75–120m. VERIFIED. [Military Times, F](https://www.militarytimes.com/pay-benefits/mil-money/2025/07/17/senators-criticize-cfpb-for-dropping-action-on-troops-overdraft-fees/); [CA AG, F](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/consumers-were-left-high-and-dry-attorney-general-bonta-secures-45-million)
5. **Overdraft fees are rising again after the rule repeal.** The CFPB measured $11.96bn in 2019 and $5.83bn in 2023. NCLC finds that the 20 largest consumer banks earned about $4bn in 2025, up 6.2% on 2023. VERIFIED for the CFPB series. The NCLC trend is REPORTED (advocacy source). [CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/data-spotlight-overdraft-nsf-revenue-in-2023-down-more-than-50-versus-pre-pandemic-levels-saving-consumers-over-6-billion-annually/); [NCLC, F](https://www.nclc.org/resources/overdraft-fees-rising-in-absence-of-cfpb-rule/)
6. **Debanking became formal policy on both sides of the Atlantic.** The UK now requires 90 days' notice plus a specific written reason for terminations, in force 28 April 2026. In the US, the OCC's October 2026 final report found that all nine of the largest banks had reputation-risk or industry-exclusion policies. The OCC was "unable to quantify" the harm, and the CFPB has opened its own debanking initiative. VERIFIED. [SI 2025/688, F](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/688/made); [OCC, F](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-90.html)
7. **Synapse customers are still owed up to $95m** two and a half years after the April 2024 collapse. The CFPB has set aside $55.2m from its Civil Penalty Fund but has given no payout date, and its claims contractor runs to August 2028. VERIFIED. [Banking Dive, F](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/synapse-small-claims-court-evolve-yotta-cfpb-customer-reimbursement/830655/)
8. **Complaints to the CBN rose from 15,385 in 2023 to 20,925 in 2024 and 23,129 in 2025** (sums of half-years). Electronic/card failures and fraud dominate. In H1 2025, 46.55% of complaints were against non-bank institutions. Complaints about excess charges rose from 78 (H2 2024) to 1,089 (H2 2025). VERIFIED. [CBN FSR Dec 2025, F](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/FPRD/FSR%20DECEMBER%202025%20EDITION%20-%20FINAL%201.07.2026.pdf)
9. **UK mandatory APP-scam reimbursement is working on its own terms.** 88% of in-scope losses (£316m) were reimbursed over 18 months, and 82% of claims were closed within 5 business days. Fraud complaints to the Financial Ombudsman fell 14% in 2025/26, from 35,416 to 30,428, which the Ombudsman partly attributes to the PSR rules. VERIFIED. [PSR, F](https://www.psr.org.uk/information-for-consumers/app-scams-reimbursement-dashboard/); [FOS AR 2025/26, F](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/files/324836/Annual-Report-and-Accounts-year-ended-31-March-2026-v2.pdf)
10. **Customer-service automation backlash is now documented.** The CFPB's June 2023 report found 37% of Americans used bank chatbots and warned of "doom loops". The Cash App order forced 24/7 live-person support. CBA reversed AI-driven job cuts as call volumes rose. Klarna's AI-efficiency claims are company-reported. VERIFIED (CFPB, CBA). Klarna: REPORTED. [CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/); [Information Age, F](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/cba-reverses-ai-driven-job-cuts--admits--error-.html)

## 3. Detailed findings by sub-topic

### 3.1 US complaint system: rising volume, contested signal
- **What happened.** Complaints to the CFPB rose from 352,400 in 2019 to 6.64m in 2025. The CFPB says volume has "roughly doubled each year" since 2023 ([CFPB 2025, F](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_2025-cr-annual-report_2026-03.pdf); [ABA Banking Journal, F](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/04/cfpb-received-6-6m-consumer-complaints-in-2025/)). Credit reporting was 85% of 2024 complaints ([Hudson Cook, F](https://www.hudsoncook.com/article/cfpbs-consumer-response-annual-report-for-2024-analyzes-increased-consumer-complaints/)).
- **Why.** The 2025 report explicitly cites "unprecedented challenges posed by credit repair and other actors" misusing the system. It also says checking and money-services complaints spiked in early 2025 "following the announcement of several CFPB enforcement matters". VERIFIED.
- **Payments-specific harm.** Money-services complaints were driven by fraud and scams in 2023 and 2024. In 2023 the CFPB noted that companies "frequently emphasized the irreversible nature of blockchain transactions". The 2025 surge is concentrated in Cash App and Zelle under "Other transaction problem". Block closed 48,063 of its 48,091 complaints in 2025 "with explanation" and only 28 with monetary relief (CFPB DB, F). INTERPRETATION: the 2025 Block/Zelle spike looks like redress-seeking and possibly templated filings after the January 2025 Cash App order and the December 2024 Zelle suit, not a sudden 25-fold rise in underlying harm. The low relief rate either reflects that or reflects Block's handling, and the data cannot tell which.
- **Supervisory posture.** By August 2026 the CFPB's supervision page lists a "Humility in Supervision Pledge" and priorities narrowed to "actual fraud... with identifiable victims", servicemember redress, mortgages and FCRA/FDCPA ([CFPB supervision, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/supervisory-highlights/)). VERIFIED.

### 3.2 US enforcement on P2P apps, cards and fintechs, and its partial reversal
- **Chime (order 7 May 2024).** $3.25m penalty plus at least $1.3m redress. Chime's policy promised refunds within 14 days of account closure, but "thousands" took more than 90 days. Chime blamed a 2020–21 vendor configuration error. Separately, Chime paid California's DFPI $2.5m in February 2024 over customer-service accuracy ([Banking Dive, F](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/chime-dinged-cfpb-delayed-customer-refunds-enforcement-action-penalty/715395/); [CFPB press release, S](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-takes-action-against-chime-financial-for-illegally-delaying-consumer-refunds)). Public Citizen notes that the partner-bank model "deprives consumers of many of the protections of banking laws" ([CLP Blog, F](https://clpblog.citizen.org/cfpb-issues-4-55-million-order-against-fintech-company-chime-for-delayed-refunds/)). Chime's published complaints rose from 3,449 (2023) to 10,237 (2026 YTD), and "Closing an account" was a top-3 issue every year (CFPB DB, F). VERIFIED.
- **Apple Card / Goldman (23 October 2024).** Apple paid $25m. Goldman paid a $45m penalty and about $20m in redress. Apple failed to forward "tens of thousands" of disputes, and Goldman did not investigate them properly ([CBS News, F](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/apple-goldman-sachs-apple-card-failures)). The Apple order was terminated in September 2025 ([Law360, F](https://www.law360.com/articles/2391489)). VERIFIED.
- **Navy Federal (7 November 2024).** More than $80m in overdraft refunds plus a $15m penalty for "authorize positive, settle negative" fees charged in 2017–2022 ([NCUA, F](https://ncua.gov/newsroom/press-release/2024/statement-chairman-harper-cfpbs-settlement-navy-federal-credit-union); [ICBA, F](https://www.icba.org/w/cfpb-penalizes-navy-federal-credit-union-for-overdraft-fees)). The order was terminated on 1 July 2025 without a stated reason. Senators warned the restitution "is now at risk" because the order lacked firm payment deadlines ([Military Times, F](https://www.militarytimes.com/pay-benefits/mil-money/2025/07/17/senators-criticize-cfpb-for-dropping-action-on-troops-overdraft-fees/)). VERIFIED. Navy Federal then became the most-complained-about checking provider in the 2025 public database (10,606).
- **Block / Cash App (16 January 2025).** Up to $175m: $75–120m in redress plus a $55m penalty. The account base was over 56m. The phone number printed on cards reached only a recorded message "for years". Users were told to ask their banks to reverse transactions and were then denied. Accounts were locked for long periods without provisional credit. Block must now provide 24/7 live-person support ([CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-orders-operator-of-cash-app-to-pay-175-million-and-fix-its-failures-on-fraud); [Engadget, F](https://www.engadget.com/cybersecurity/cfpb-fines-block-175m-over-cash-apps-lax-fraud-controls-210749768.html)). On 8 July 2026, 46 attorneys general settled with Block for $45m. The settlement explicitly binds Block to the CFPB redress "as a backstop against termination" of the federal order ([CA AG, F](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/consumers-were-left-high-and-dry-attorney-general-bonta-secures-45-million)). VERIFIED.
- **Zelle.** The CFPB sued Early Warning Services, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo in December 2024, citing more than $870m in losses. It dismissed the suit with prejudice on 4 March 2025 ([Crowdfund Insider, S](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/03/237009-cfpb-drops-zelle-fraud-lawsuit-against-major-us-banking-institutions/)). The New York AG sued Early Warning on 13 August 2025, alleging more than $1bn stolen in 2017–2023 and safeguards developed in 2019 but never adopted. Zelle says 99.95% of transactions have no scam report ([American Banker, F](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/ny-ag-sues-zelle-for-fraud-after-cfpb-dropped-similar-suit)). DISPUTED (litigation ongoing).
- **Synapse (collapsed April 2024).** Partner banks held about $180m against $265m owed to end users. More than 100,000 users were locked out, including about 85,000 Yotta customers ([Banking Dive, F](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/synapse-85-million-shortfall-partner-banks-mcwilliams/718796/)). The CFPB obtained a $1 judgment (entered 12 September 2025) to unlock its Civil Penalty Fund ([CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/synapse-financial-technologies-inc/); [American Banker, F](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/synapse-bankruptcy-dismissed-cfpb-may-start-paying-victims)). The bankruptcy was dismissed in November 2025. As of 17 September 2026, $55.2m had been allocated but nothing paid out. Victims are winning small-claims cases against Evolve one by one ([Banking Dive, F](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/synapse-small-claims-court-evolve-yotta-cfpb-customer-reimbursement/830655/)). VERIFIED.
- **Rule repeals.** On 9 May 2025, Congressional Review Act resolutions voided the CFPB overdraft rule (a $5 cap for banks over $10bn, due 1 October 2025) and the November 2024 larger-participant rule for digital payment apps. Under the CRA, the CFPB cannot reissue a substantially similar rule ([Ballard Spahr, S](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2025/05/president-trump-signs-congressional-review-act-resolution-overturning-cfpb-overdraft-rule/)). VERIFIED by multiple secondary sources.

### 3.3 Fees: overdraft and "junk" fees (US) and bank charges (Nigeria)
- **US.** Overdraft/NSF revenue at banks over $1bn in assets was $11.96bn (2019), $7.61bn (2022) and $5.83bn (2023) ([CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/data-spotlight-overdraft-nsf-revenue-in-2023-down-more-than-50-versus-pre-pandemic-levels-saving-consumers-over-6-billion-annually/)). 27 of the top 30 earners had dropped NSF fees by 2023, but 16 of 20 credit unions over $10bn still charged them ([CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/vast-majority-of-nsf-fees-have-been-eliminated-saving-consumers-nearly-2-billion-annually/)). After the repeal, NCLC reports the top 20 consumer banks earned about $4bn in 2025 (+6.2% vs 2023), including Huntington +40% and M&T +36%. BMO cut its fee to $15, then raised it to $20 in 2026. The NCUA stopped collecting credit-union overdraft data ([NCLC, F](https://www.nclc.org/resources/overdraft-fees-rising-in-absence-of-cfpb-rule/)). JPMorgan's overdraft income rose 7.66% year on year in the first nine months of 2025 ([American Banker, S](https://americanbanker.com/news/overdraft-fee-income-is-on-the-rise-at-these-big-banks)). REPORTED. Checking-account complaints tagged "Problem caused by your funds being low" rose from 3,307 (2023) to 11,939 (2025) (CFPB DB, F). VERIFIED (data). The link to fees is an INTERPRETATION.
- **Nigeria.** The CBN's April 2026 draft *Guide to Charges* would make transfers under ₦5,000 free and charge ₦10 for ₦5k–50k. But stamp duty was reintroduced and shifted to the sender, so transfers over ₦10,000 still cost at least ₦60 ([CBN draft, F](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/Signed%20Guide%20to%20Charges%20April%202026.pdf); [TechCabal, F](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/27/why-transfers-above-%e2%82%a610000-will-cost-%e2%82%a660/)). "Excess charges" complaints to the CBN rose from 81 (H1 2024) to 1,089 (H2 2025). VERIFIED.

### 3.4 Account restrictions, frozen funds and debanking
**UK**
- *Coutts/Farage (July 2023).* NatWest's CEO resigned and lost about £7.6m. NatWest apologised and settled confidentially in March 2025 ([FStech, S](https://fstech.co.uk/fst/NatWest_And_Nigel_Farage_Settle_Long_Running_Debanking_Controversy.php)). The FCA's September 2023 review of 34 firms found no closures (July 2022–June 2023) made primarily for political views. Dormancy and financial-crime concerns dominated ([Regulation Tomorrow, F](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2023/09/fca-publishes-initial-findings-on-bank-account-access-and-closures/)). The September 2024 follow-up found closures driven by "non-specific financial crime concerns" (including the cost of enhanced due diligence), loosely defined "reputational risk", rejection of alternative ID for vulnerable customers, and thin record-keeping ([FCA, F](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/corporate/uk-payment-accounts-access-closures-update.pdf)). The APPG on Fair Business Banking cites FCA figures of about 185,000 accounts closed for financial-crime reasons in 2022–23, against about 72,000 in 2017–18 ([GB News, F](https://www.gbnews.com/money/debanking-banks-cost-reputation)). REPORTED.
- *Rule change.* SI 2025/688 (made 12 June 2025, in force **28 April 2026**) requires at least 90 days' notice for indefinite framework contracts entered on or after that date. The notice must explain the reasons "sufficiently detailed and specific" for the customer to understand. There are financial-crime exceptions ([legislation.gov.uk, F](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/688/made); [HM Treasury, F](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/millions-of-people-and-businesses-protected-against-debanking)). VERIFIED. FOS account-closure complaints were already rising (2,708 in 2022/23, +7%), and 2023/24 media attention increased them further (FOS ARs, F).
- *Neobank growth versus controls.* The FCA fined Starling £28.96m (October 2024; customers grew from 43,000 to 3.6m) and Monzo £21.09m (July 2025; customers grew from 600,000 to 5.8m) for financial-crime controls that lagged growth ([FCA Starling, F](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-starling-bank-failings-financial-crime-systems-and-controls); [FCA Monzo, F](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-monzo-21m-failings-financial-crime-controls)). INTERPRETATION: remediation after enforcement is a known trigger for waves of account freezes and exits.

**US**
- *"Operation Choke Point 2.0."* FDIC letters (2022–23) asked banks to "pause" crypto activity but did not expressly order them to stop serving crypto firms ([Banking Dive, F](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/unredacted-letters-show-fdic-asked-for-crypto-pause-but-not-debanking/736559/)). In 2025 the FDIC settled a related FOIA fight and paid $188,440 in fees ([Decrypt, S](https://decrypt.co/357422/fdic-pay-fees-drop-foia-crypto-pause-letters?amp=1)). DISPUTED.
- *Executive Order 14331 (August 2025) and follow-up.* The OCC's preliminary findings (December 2025) said the nine largest national banks made "inappropriate distinctions" on lawful business activities in 2020–23 ([OCC, F](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-123.html)). The final report (14 October 2026) covered 2020 to September 2025. It found that all nine had policies restricting oil and gas, coal, firearms, detention facilities, tobacco and digital assets, and it reviewed about 100,000 complaints. But the OCC was "unable to quantify" the extent of debanking and cited no violation of law ([OCC, F](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-90.html); [NatLaw Review, F](https://natlawreview.com/article/occ-final-report-debanking-focuses-subjective-risk-practices-and-signals-continued)). On 7 April 2026 the OCC and FDIC finalised a rule barring supervisors from using "reputation risk" ([FDIC Chair statement, F](https://www.fdic.gov/statement-chairman-travis-hill-final-rule-remove-reputational-risk-fdics-supervisory-program-pdf); [Ballard Spahr, S](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/04/15/fdic-occ-adopt-debanking-final-rule/)). On 14 October 2026 the CFPB launched a debanking initiative under ECOA, FCRA and UDAAP and redesigned its complaint portal to invite debanking complaints ([CFPB, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-announces-new-initiative-to-address-occ-uncovered-debanking-by-large-banks/)). VERIFIED (actions). The scale of harm is DISPUTED.

**Nigeria / Africa**
- *April 2024 freezes.* A Federal High Court order on 24 April 2024 let the EFCC block 1,146 accounts over alleged unauthorised FX and crypto peer-to-peer dealing. Banks emailed crypto traders demanding court orders to release funds, and 90% of the accounts were at traditional banks ([TechCabal, F](https://techcabal.com/2024/04/26/efcc-blocks-1146-accounts/)). The CBN then halted onboarding at OPay, Palmpay, Kuda and Moniepoint for about six weeks, until early June 2024 ([Nairametrics, F](https://nairametrics.com/2024/04/29/forex-deals-cbn-stops-4-fintechs-from-onboarding-new-customers/); [The Condia, F](https://thecondia.com/nigerian-fintechs-resume-customer-enrollment-after-crackdown/)). In September 2024, a further order froze ₦548.6m in 22 accounts of USDT sellers ([Nairametrics, F](https://nairametrics.com/2024/09/11/court-freezes-n548-6-million-belonging-to-bybit-kucoin-nigerian-crypto-users-over-naira-fluctuation-allegations/?amp=1)). The causes are DISPUTED (FX manipulation versus KYC failings; see §4).
- *De-risking of correspondent banking.* BIS data show global correspondent relationships fell about a quarter between 2011 and 2022. The contraction slowed in 2024–25 but now shows up as incremental due-diligence demands and "informal volume guidance" ([Trade Treasury Payments, F](https://tradetreasurypayments.com/articles/engineering-resilience-structural-concentration-and-the-future-of-usd-correspondent-banking-in-emerging-markets)). Historic baseline: South Africa lost more than 10% of its foreign counterparties in 2013–15, and Angola lost 37% ([Financial Nigeria/SWIFT, F](https://www.financialnigeria.com/de-risking-in-africa-correspondent-banking-on-the-decline-feature-75.html)). The EBA's 2024/25 Consumer Trends Report ranks de-risking as the #3 EU consumer issue, hitting "migrants, refugees, the homeless, cross-border workers" ([EBA, F](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-identifies-payment-fraud-indebtedness-and-de-risking-key-issues-affecting-consumers-eu)). VERIFIED.
- *Foreign fintechs restricting Nigerian users (Wise, Payoneer, Grey, Chipper) and the Flutterwave Kenya freeze:* **not verified in this slice** (see the coverage audit).

### 3.5 Disputes, chargebacks and friendly fraud
- **Card networks tightened.** Visa merged its fraud and dispute monitoring programmes into VAMP in April 2025. From April 2026, a merchant is "excessive" at a 1.5% ratio (2.2% in CEMEA) and an acquirer at 0.7%. A third of merchants surveyed did not know whether VAMP affects them ([Chargebacks911, F](https://chargebacks911.com/vamp/)). Visa Compelling Evidence 3.0 (updated April 2026; launch date not verified here) lets merchants rebut "fraud – card absent" (reason code 10.4) disputes using prior undisputed transactions ([Chargebacks911, F](https://chargebacks911.com/compelling-evidence-3-0/)). REPORTED (vendor source; Visa's own pages were not reachable).
- **Volumes.** Vendor-compiled figures put global chargebacks at 238m (2023) rising to a forecast 337m (2026). Merchants win 43.8% of represented disputes but net only 10.7% recovery. 72% of cardholders see disputes as an alternative to refunds (Ethoca) ([Chargebacks911, F](https://chargebacks911.com/chargeback-stats/)). REPORTED (vendor). I could not reach Mastercard's *State of Chargebacks* (the host was blocked).
- **Consumer side.** The Apple Card and Cash App orders show the mirror image: legitimate disputes not forwarded or not investigated, and customers redirected to their banks. ECC-Net is calling for chargeback rights beyond credit cards in the EU ([ECC-Net, F](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/47efcd6d-eb6a-4564-a873-077d11c5f7c0_en?filename=ECC_Net_report_2023_0%20%285%29.pdf)). The UK closed the APP gap through regulation (§3.6). Nigeria drafted equivalent rules (§3.7). INTERPRETATION: the industry is squeezed both ways. Networks penalise merchants for friendly fraud, while regulators penalise providers for under-investigating genuine disputes.

### 3.6 Fraud reimbursement outcomes (UK; contrast with US and EU)
- PSR mandatory reimbursement started on 7 October 2024. Over 18 months it reimbursed 88% (£316m) of in-scope losses. Of about 438,300 claims, 301,500 were in scope, 82% were closed within 5 business days, and 3% were rejected for lack of consumer caution ([PSR dashboard, F](https://www.psr.org.uk/information-for-consumers/app-scams-reimbursement-dashboard/)). On 1 July 2026 the PSR reported that reimbursement of all claims rose from 54% to 65%, scams fell by nearly 35,000 and losses fell by about £73m a year ([PSR, F](https://www.psr.org.uk/news-and-updates/latest-news/news/payment-fraud-falls-by-73m-following-psr-reimbursement-scheme/)). VERIFIED.
- The US has no equivalent. Zelle scam reimbursement remains voluntary and is litigated. In the EU, the EBA/ECB say payer-manipulation fraud is "on the rise" even though EEA fraud is only about 0.002% of value (€4.2bn in 2024). I did not verify the status of EU legislative reform on reimbursement in this slice ([EBA-ECB, F](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/joint-eba-ecb-report-payment-fraud-strong-authentication-remains-effective-fraudsters-are-adapting)).

### 3.7 Failed transactions, refunds and reversal timelines (Nigeria focus)
- Failed transactions were 45.2% of CBN complaints in H1 2023, during the naira-redesign cash crunch. Claims that half-year reached ₦31.27bn, of which ₦15.63bn was refunded (CBN FSR June 2023, F). Electronic/card complaints were still 36.6% in H2 2025.
- **New timelines:**
  - *Draft ATM guidelines (October 2025):* failed on-us reversals must be instant, with manual reversal within 24 hours at most; not-on-us refunds within 48 hours; acquirers must refund "without the prompting" of the issuer or customer ([CBN, F](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/DRAFT%20GUIDELINES%20ON%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20AUTOMATED%20TELLER%20MACHINES%20(ATMs)%20IN%20NIGERIA%2009102025.pdf)).
  - *Draft APP fraud guidelines (1 December 2025):* notify the counterparty institution within 30 minutes; reimburse within 48 hours of a concluded investigation and within 16 working days in total; institutions whose detection is too weak to flag or freeze proceeds are "debited for the total exposure"; no-fault cases are split 50/50 ([CBN, F](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/Exposure%20draft%20of%20the%20Guidelines%20for%20Handling%20Authorised%20Push%20Payment%20Fraud.pdf)).
  - *Draft CBN–NCC framework for failed airtime and data purchases (9 February 2026):* automated reversal SLAs (for example, reversing pending stock within 6 hours) and escalation to regulators after 5 working days ([CBN, F](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/Exposure%20Draft%20of%20CBN_NCC%20Framework%20on%20Failed%20Airtime%20and%20Data%20Purchase%20Transactions%20(2).pdf)).
  - The CBN issued its "Industry Dispute Resolution System – Notification of Go-Live" circular on 27 February 2024 ([CBN circulars API, F](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/api/GetAllCirculars)).
  - VERIFIED. Most of these are **exposure drafts**, and I did not find final versions.

### 3.8 Outages
- **UK.** Treasury Committee data show that nine lenders had at least 158 IT incidents between January 2023 and February 2025, totalling more than 33 days of outages. Barclays' late-January 2025 mainframe-software failure (at a third-party provider) caused more than half of online payments to fail around payday and the tax deadline. Barclays expects £5–7.5m in compensation for that incident plus about £5m for others ([Evening Standard, F](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/tech/barclays-meg-hillier-mps-b1214984.html)). VERIFIED (cites committee letters; the committee site itself was blocked).
- **Global.** The CrowdStrike update on 19 July 2024 hit about 8.5m Windows devices. Bank apps were disrupted at Chase, BofA, Wells, Capitec, Bradesco and Australian banks ([Microsoft, F](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2024/07/20/helping-our-customers-through-the-crowdstrike-outage/); [Wikipedia, F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_CrowdStrike-related_IT_outages)).
- **PayPal Germany (August 2025).** PayPal's fraud filters failed and direct debits were submitted unchecked. German banks, including BayernLB, Helaba and DZ Bank, blocked PayPal debits possibly worth more than €10bn, and the backlog had to be processed manually ([The Local, F](https://www.thelocal.de/20250827/paypal-direct-debit-glitch-causes-major-payment-delays-in-germany); [heise, F](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Paypal-German-banks-apparently-blocked-payments-of-billions-of-euros-10622936.html)). VERIFIED.
- **Kenya.** In January 2026 the CBK told Parliament that an M-Pesa failure would "significantly impair the real economy". M-Pesa processed KES 83.7trn in 2025 (about 4x GDP) and holds KES 250bn in trust accounts, and Safaricom has a 95% share of retail payments ([TechCabal, F](https://techcabal.com/2026/01/28/kenya-central-bank-m-pesa-failure-economy-collapse/)).

### 3.9 Customer-service automation
- **CFPB issue spotlight (6 June 2023).** About 98m users (37% of the US population) used a bank chatbot in 2022, and all top-10 banks use them. The CFPB warned that "doom loops" without a route to a human, and bots that fail to recognise when a consumer is invoking legal dispute rights, may breach the law ([CFPB report, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/); [press release, F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-issue-spotlight-analyzes-artificial-intelligence-chatbots-in-banking/)).
- **Klarna (27 February 2024).** Klarna said its AI handled 2.3m chats (two-thirds of the total), cut resolution time from 11 minutes to 2, did the work of 700 full-time agents and was worth an estimated $40m in profit ([Klarna, F](https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/)). Headcount fell from 5,527 (2022) to 2,907 (Guardian via [Wikipedia, F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klarna)). Klarna's figures are self-reported (REPORTED). Reports that Klarna later re-hired human agents were **not verified** here.
- **CBA (August 2025).** CBA reversed the replacement of 45 roles with a "voice bot" and admitted an "error". The union said call volumes were rising and staff were being offered overtime ([Information Age, F](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2025/cba-reverses-ai-driven-job-cuts--admits--error-.html)). VERIFIED.
- **Cash App.** The CFPB order mandated 24/7 live-person support after years of a recorded-message phone line. INTERPRETATION: regulators now treat reachable humans as a compliance requirement for dispute handling, not a cost line.

### 3.10 Complaint-handling quality under the UK Consumer Duty
The Duty applied from 31 July 2023 to open products and from 31 July 2024 to closed products. In a July 2026 review of 40 firms, the FCA found that complaint data was often not granular enough to show outcomes for vulnerable customers, was not acted on at decision-making forums, and that firms did not measure whether their fixes worked ([FCA, F](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/complaints-and-root-cause-analysis-good-practice-and-areas-improvement)). The FCA says it is "relying on the Duty as much as possible rather than creating new prescriptive rules" and is reviewing customer-journey "friction" ([FCA focus areas, F](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/corporate-documents/consumer-duty-focus-areas)), while simplifying its rules (FS25/2, [F](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/feedback-statements/fs25-2-action-plans-reviewing-requirements-consumer-duty)).

## 4. Contradictions and disputed claims
1. **How much debanking is there, and why?** The OCC found exclusion policies at all nine banks but "could not quantify" the harm and cited no violation. The FCA found no political closures. The UK APPG argues that cost and profitability, not crime, drive closures. Crypto firms point to the FDIC "pause letters", but the unredacted letters do not order debanking. DISPUTED.
2. **Why Nigeria halted fintech onboarding in April 2024.** Nairametrics' sources tied it to illicit FX. Techeconomy's sources said it was "largely KYC-related", following the CBN's 31 January 2024 KYC deadline. Fintechs said 90% of the implicated accounts were at commercial banks, which faced no onboarding ban ([Techeconomy, F](https://www.techeconomy.ng/kyc-as-major-reason-cbn-ordered-four-fintech-startups-to-halt-customer-onboarding); [BusinessDay, F](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/four-fintechs-suspend-account-opening-on-kyc-issues)). DISPUTED.
3. **The EFCC freeze framing.** DL News says "1,146 crypto traders". TechCabal and the court frame it as unauthorised FX, money laundering and terrorism financing ([DL News, F](https://dlnews.com/articles/regulation/nigeria-efcc-crackdown-crypto-traders-merchants/)). DISPUTED.
4. **Overdraft totals.** The CFPB reported $5.83bn (2023, banks over $1bn). NCLC reports about $12.1–12.4bn (2024–25), but that figure is an extrapolation to all banks and credit unions using FinHealth data. The scopes differ, so the figures cannot be compared directly. Secondary sources give the top-20 growth as either 4.2% or 6.2% [S]. I use the 6.2% that I read on NCLC's own page.
5. **Zelle's safety.** NY AG: more than $1bn stolen. Zelle: 99.95% of transactions have no scam report. Both can be true at Zelle's scale (over $1trn in 2024). DISPUTED as to fault.
6. **What CFPB complaint spikes mean.** Is it genuine harm (as the enforcement announcements suggest) or misuse of the system (as the 2025 report warns about credit-repair firms)? Block's 0.06% monetary-relief rate in 2025 is consistent with either reading. DISPUTED.
7. **The size of the Synapse hole.** Estimates are $65m to $95m, while the trustee's mid-2024 figure was $85m. Estimates differ by source and date.
8. **Ombudsman totals.** FOS 2025/26 is 214,872 in the annual report and 214,600 on the insight page. India's FY24 total is 934,355 in the FY24 report and 1,175,075 as restated in the FY25 report. The differences come from rounding and changed definitions.

## 5. How the issues evolved, 2023 to 2026

| Year | Dominant customer-harm themes | Regulatory direction |
|---|---|---|
| **2023** | Fraud and scams via P2P and crypto. NSF fees largely abolished in the US. Nigeria's naira redesign made failed transactions 45% of CBN complaints. Coutts/Farage made "debanking" mainstream. Chatbot doom loops. | Expansionary: CFPB chatbot and junk-fee work, UK Consumer Duty (July), FCA access review (September). |
| **2024** | Synapse frozen funds (100k+ users). Delayed refunds (Chime). Dispute failures (Apple Card). Overdraft (Navy Federal). Nigeria EFCC freezes and fintech onboarding ban. CrowdStrike outage. | Peak enforcement: CFPB orders, larger-participant rule (November), Zelle suit (December). UK APP reimbursement (October). CBN dispute system and e-escalation form. |
| **2025** | Complaint volumes explode (US 6.6m, UK FOS 305,918 in 2024/25). Cash App/Zelle complaint waves. Barclays and PayPal-Germany outages. AI service backlash (CBA). Overdraft income rising again. | US federal reversal: Zelle suit dropped, orders terminated, overdraft and P2P rules voided, EO 14331 on debanking. States backfill (NY AG). UK finalises 90-day/reasons rule. CBN drafts APP and ATM rules. |
| **2026** | PayPal and Chime complaints at record highs. Synapse victims still unpaid. Fees under review in Nigeria. UK fraud complaints falling. Brazil's complaints at around 300k a quarter. | The debanking agenda turns against banks (OCC/FDIC rule in April, OCC report and CFPB initiative in October). UK 90-day rule in force (28 April). 46-AG Cash App settlement (July). FCA relies on the Duty plus simplification. |

INTERPRETATION: the trend lines diverge. Where a regulator **mandated an outcome** (UK APP reimbursement, Nigeria escalation deadlines), complaints have stabilised or fallen and refunds have risen. Where federal mandates were **withdrawn** (US overdraft, P2P), fees and complaints rose, and enforcement moved to the states. The meaning of "debanking" also flipped. It started as a consumer-protection and inclusion concern (EBA: migrants and refugees; FCA: vulnerable customers). In the US it became a political and industry-access concern (firearms, oil, crypto) with new legal tools.

## 6. Implications for PayPal and the wider industry
- **PayPal (INTERPRETATION on VERIFIED data).** PayPal's CFPB complaints rose 2.9x from 2023 to 2026 YTD. The growth is in "unauthorized transactions" (2,283 YTD) and "trouble accessing funds" (1,903 YTD), which are the categories that best match account holds and limitations. Unlike Block (under 1%), PayPal closes 11–20% of complaints with monetary relief (13% in 2023, 11% in 2024, 13% in 2025, 19.6% in 2026 YTD). This suggests real, remediable failures rather than template filings. The volume is a supervisory and reputational risk. The Cash App precedent shows a P2P/wallet provider can be ordered to provide 24/7 human support and full Reg E investigations, and **state AGs can enforce even when the CFPB retreats**. PayPal's Germany direct-debit failure shows that fraud-control outages become counterparty and bank-relationship crises. In the UK, the 90-day notice-with-reasons regime applies to payment service providers under PSRs 2017, so any UK-regulated PayPal entity terminating such contracts falls within it. Unexplained, abrupt "limitations" will be harder to defend there.
- **Wider industry.** (1) Fraud-reimbursement liability is being shifted onto providers by rule: UK in force, Nigeria drafted. In the EU, the EBA ranks payment fraud as the #1 consumer issue. (2) Explainability of account actions is now a legal duty in the UK and a political target in the US. (3) BaaS ledgers need regulator-grade reconciliation, as the Synapse shortfall shows. (4) AI customer service must have guaranteed routes to a human. (5) Third-party concentration (CrowdStrike, Barclays' mainframe vendor, M-Pesa) is a source of consumer harm, not only an operational risk.

## 7. Unresolved problems as of November 2026 (evidence-backed)
1. **Synapse:** up to $95m still owed to end users and no CFPB payout date ([Banking Dive, Sept 2026, F](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/synapse-small-claims-court-evolve-yotta-cfpb-customer-reimbursement/830655/)).

9. **PayPal's complaint trajectory:** still rising through September 2026.

## 8. Opportunity signals (all SPECULATIVE)
1. **Complaint and dispute operations for African fintechs.** Evidence: CBN complaints up 50% since 2023, with non-banks at 46.55% of H1 2025. The draft rules require 30-minute counterparty notice, 48-hour reimbursement and 16-day resolution. Product idea: a shared case-management and inter-institution messaging layer, effectively an IDRS-compatible "dispute switch" for mid-sized fintechs and microfinance banks.
2. **Auto-reversal reconciliation as a service.** Evidence: failed transactions reached 45% of CBN complaints, and the draft ATM and airtime frameworks mandate instant or 24-hour reversals. Product idea: real-time reconciliation and automated reversal triggering for acquirers, mobile operators and aggregators.

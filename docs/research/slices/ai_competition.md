### 2.2 Big banks vs regionals; M&A and concentration

**JPMorgan's trajectory (SEC 8-K exhibits, [F]):**
- **2023:** $49.6bn. Dimon said the record results "reflect over-earning on both NII and credit."
- **2024:** a record $58.5bn, or $54.0bn excluding significant items such as the Visa share gain.
- **2025:** $57.0bn, ROTCE 20%, after a $2.2bn reserve for taking over the Apple Card portfolio.
- **Q3 2026:** $15.3bn on $50.2bn of revenue; overhead ratio 54%; #1 in US retail deposits for the sixth year.
- In Q1 2023, JPMorgan held 11.2% of US deposits, before it bought First Republic ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/ja/node/7956651) [S]). The four largest banks hold about 40% of US deposits ([BizTimes citing FDIC](https://biztimes.com/?p=409557) [S]).
- VERIFIED (earnings); REPORTED (concentration shares).

**The M&A wave.**
- In 2025 the OCC and FDIC withdrew the merger policy statements adopted under Biden. Approvals averaged about 4 months, the fastest since 1990, down from nearly 7 months under the previous administration. About 150 deals worth about $45bn closed by November 2025 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/bank-regulation/2025/bank-merger-approval-rates-hit-35-year-high/) [F]; [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/2026-insights/sector-spotlights/the-long-anticipated-wave-of-bank-consolidation) [F]).
- At least 170 deals were announced in 2025 ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/the-five-biggest-bank-m-a-deals-of-2025) [S]).
- Landmark deals:
  - Capital One–Discover ($35.5bn), approved April 2025. Discover was fined $100m for overcharging interchange fees from 2007 to 2023 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/orders20250418a.htm) [F]).
  - Fifth Third–Comerica ($10.9bn), closed in under four months ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/fifth-third-closes-comerica-acquisition-in-under-four-months) [S]).
  - Santander–Webster ($12.3bn, 2026) ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/banco-santander-inks-12-3b-deal-to-buy-webster-financial) [S]).
- Why it is happening: deregulation, scale economics in technology (JPMorgan's 2026 tech budget is about **$19.8bn** [F]), shareholder activism, and the 2023 deposit flight to perceived "too-big-to-fail" banks.
- Who is affected: community banks and local lending. The number of FDIC-insured institutions keeps falling.

**Business-model lesson (INTERPRETATION).** The challengers that made money did three things:
1. Own a licence: Nubank, Revolut, Monzo, SoFi.
2. Monetise deposits and lending as well as interchange.
3. Run at very low cost-to-income (Nubank 19.9% against 51–61% at incumbents).

Interchange-only or BNPL-only models (Chime, Klarna) earn thin profits. That is why both are adding lending, subscriptions, charters and stablecoins.

### 2.4 Payments competition

**Card networks.**
- **DOJ v Visa:** DOJ sued on 24 Sept 2024, alleging monopolisation of debit and card-not-present debit. The court denied Visa's motion to dismiss on 23 June 2025 ([Visa 10-K FY25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000089/v-20250930.htm) [F]).
- **Debit fee cap (Regulation II):** in August 2025 a North Dakota court vacated the Fed's debit interchange standard, but a Kentucky court upheld the Fed. The Credit Card Competition Act "may be reintroduced." Illinois restricted interchange on the tax and tip portions of transactions in May 2024 [F].
- **Merchant class action (MDL 1720):** Visa and Mastercard signed a superseding injunctive-relief settlement on 10 Nov 2025, preliminarily approved on 9 June 2026; a motion for final approval was filed on 15 July 2026. In Europe, new merchant claims were filed in the UK High Court in April–June 2026 ([Visa 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000104/v-20260630.htm) [F]).
- Visa itself says it is pursuing about $20tn of spend still moving through cash, cheques, ACH, account-to-account (A2A) and real-time payments [F].
- VERIFIED.

**Wallets and A2A.**
- The EU made Apple's commitments binding on 11 July 2024. Third-party wallets in the EEA get free NFC access via host card emulation (HCE), can be set as the default payment app, and Apple must run fair, transparent access criteria and a dispute mechanism ([EC IP/24/3706](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_24_3706) [F]).
- *Not verified:* the US opening of NFC in iOS 18.1.
- **Wero (EPI):**
  - 14m users by November 2024 [Wikipedia F]; more than 44m customers within a year [BNP Paribas F].
  - E-commerce payments since November 2025, and Deutsche Bank launched it in December 2025 ([Deutsche Bank](https://www.db.com/news/detail/20251217-deutsche-bank-launches-wero-for-more-simple-and-sovereign-digital-payments-in-europe?language_id=1) [F]).
  - The iDEAL→Wero migration started in January 2026, and in-store payments started in Luxembourg in September 2026 [F].

Lloyds puts GenAI value at about £50m in 2025 and more than £100m in 2026 ([Evident](https://evidentinsights.com/ai-index/) [F]; [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-10-07-evident-ai-index-hsbc-drops-out-top-ten/) [F]).

## 4. Contradictions and disputed claims

1. **McKinsey ROE series is not comparable across editions.** GBAR 2023 gave a 12% ROE for 2022, while GBAR 2025 calls 10.3% in 2024 a "record." McKinsey has noted that earlier editions included non-intermediary institutions [S]. Do not chain these numbers together.
2. **KPMG revised H2 2025.** The February 2026 report gave $56.3bn; the August 2026 report gives $72.2bn [F both]. KPMG ($116bn, including M&A) and CB Insights ($52.7bn, equity only) also measure different things.
3. **CBA call volumes.** The bank said they fell by 2,000 a week; the union said they rose [F].
4. **Klarna.** It claimed AI replaced 700 agents, then rehired some humans [F].
5. **AI and jobs.** Evident says AI leaders are adding staff, while Block, Barclays and HSBC are cutting [F].
6. **Revolut's valuation.** $75bn (funding round), $121.1bn (Euronews, October 2026) and a $150–200bn IPO target (FT via TechCrunch) [F].
7. **Who bought Worldpay.** KPMG says Global Payments; InvestmentNews says FIS [F both]. KPMG is likely correct.
8. **Scope of Nigeria's windfall tax.** Sources differ on 50% vs 70%, realised vs revaluation gains, and 2023 only vs 2023–25 [F]. The enacted text is 70% of realised gains from 2023 to 2025.
9. **Regulation II.** Courts in North Dakota and Kentucky reached opposite rulings [F].

## 5. How the issues evolved, 2023 → 2026

- **2023: the rate windfall.** Margins widened, US regional banks failed and paid special assessments, Nigeria's FX revaluation gains arrived, and fintech funding collapsed.
- **2024: the year of reckoning.** Fintech funding hit a seven-year low and Synapse collapsed. Regulators set rules: the CFPB's 1033 rule, the EU commitments forcing Apple to open NFC, DOJ v Visa, the FSB, BoE and Treasury AI reports, and the EU AI Act. Klarna made its AI claims. Nigeria passed the windfall tax.
- **2025: deregulation and listings.** US merger approvals accelerated; Chime, Klarna and Circle listed. The agentic-commerce protocols launched. The CFPB reopened 1033. CBA reversed its AI cuts. Nigerian FX gains faded.
- **2026: consolidation at scale.** Fintech funding of $103bn came mostly through M&A (Worldpay). Fintechs moved to become banks. Block made deep AI-justified cuts. Stripe bid for PayPal. Agentic traffic grew, Evident recorded the fastest adoption pace yet, and cloud concentration caused real outages.

## 6. Implications for PayPal and the wider industry

**PayPal (INTERPRETATION):**
- Stripe has scale (TPV $1.9tn, valuation $159bn), owns Paystack in Africa, co-created the ACP and bought OpenRouter. It is setting the standards for agentic checkout and now bids for PayPal itself. Whatever the bid's outcome, PayPal's independence and strategy are in question.
- In Europe, Wero (more than 44m users) and Apple's NFC opening reduce the advantage of PayPal's wallet. EPI explicitly targets the intermediaries PayPal competes alongside.
- PayPal is an AP2 participant and bought Cymbio. Being a participant is not the same as controlling a standard.
- Block's 40% cut resets cost expectations across the peer group.

**Industry:**
- Profits are at records, but they depend on margins and markets, and costs are rigid (EU cost-to-income above 51%).
- AI is the main lever for cost-to-income and the main threat to deposit inertia (McKinsey's $170bn at risk).
- Scale players (JPMorgan with a ~$19.8bn tech budget; the North American AI leaders) are pulling away. That will speed up M&A among mid-sized banks.

## 7. Unresolved problems as of November 2026 (evidence-backed)

1. **BaaS reconciliation.** Synapse victims were still pursuing recovery in 2025 [Fortune F]. No complete fix for ledger fragmentation between fintechs and sponsor banks is evident.
2. **US open banking is in limbo.** Rule 1033 is under reconsideration, including whether banks can charge fees [F], and access is now negotiated bilaterally [JPM F].
3. **Interchange.** The US class settlement still awaits final approval and is being contested by merchant objectors. DOJ v Visa is pending. The Regulation II debit cap is subject to conflicting rulings [F].
4. **AI accountability.** 46% of UK firms only partly understand their AI [BoE F]. AI savings claims are disputed (CBA, Klarna). Only 12 of 50 large banks quantify AI ROI [F].
5. **Third-party concentration.** Three providers hold 73% of UK firms' cloud provision [BoE F], and the Revolut/Google Cloud outage showed the risk is live [F].
6. **Rigid costs at incumbents.** EU cost-to-income has been flat at about 52–53% since 2023 [EBA F].
7. **Neobank compliance capacity.** The ECB restricted Revolut's product launches [F].
8. **Nigerian bank earnings quality.** The FX windfall has gone, the 70% levy is now in accounts, and banks face recapitalisation (INTERPRETATION; FY2025 data not obtained).

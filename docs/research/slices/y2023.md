## 1. Key findings (top 10)

1. **2023 was the worst banking stress since 2008, and it hit through deposit runs, not credit losses.** The BCBS called it "the most significant system-wide banking stress since the Great Financial Crisis". VERIFIED: [BCBS report, 5 Oct 2023 [F]](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d555.htm); [FSB, 10 Oct 2023 [F]](https://www.fsb.org/2023/10/2023-bank-failures-preliminary-lessons-learnt-for-resolution/).
2. **Runs moved at app speed.** SVB lost >$40bn on 9 Mar and expected >$100bn more on 10 Mar, about 85% of deposits. WaMu lost $19bn over 16 days in 2008. The Fed said social media plus technology "may have fundamentally changed the speed of bank runs". VERIFIED: [Fed SVB review [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf).
3. **Every official post-mortem blamed supervisors as well as managers.** SVB had 31 unaddressed supervisory findings, about triple its peers. The Fed blamed its own "tailoring" and its less assertive supervisory culture. VERIFIED: [Fed press release [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230428a.htm); [FDIC Signature review [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23033.html); [GAO-23-106736 [F]](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106736).
4. **Rate risk was the hidden fuse.** US banks' unrealised securities losses were $515.5bn (Q1), $558.4bn (Q2), about $684bn (Q3) and $477.6bn (Q4 2023). VERIFIED: [FDIC QBP Q1 [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23043.html), [Q2 [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23072.html), [Q4 [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2024/pr24014.html).
5. **The public bill was large, and the "no bailout" framing is debatable.** Estimated deposit insurance fund (DIF) costs were about $20bn for SVB, $2.5bn for Signature and $13bn for First Republic. A $16.3bn special assessment on 114 large banks recovers the cost of protecting uninsured depositors. The Swiss state backstopped up to CHF 100bn+ of liquidity to save Credit Suisse, and about CHF 16bn of AT1 bonds were wiped out. VERIFIED: [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23023.html), [FDIC fact sheet [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/fact-sheets/special-assessment-final-rule-11-07-23.pdf), [FINMA [F]](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2023/03/20230319-mm-cs-ubs/).
6. **Regulators proposed sweeping reforms in 2023, and most were reversed or stalled by 2025–26.** Examples: Basel endgame (+16% CET1), the CFPB's 1033 open-banking rule, oversight of payment apps, the $8 late-fee cap, and the Fed's novel-activities programme. VERIFIED: [Fed Basel [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230727a.htm); [CFPB 1033 [F]](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-proposes-rule-to-jumpstart-competition-and-accelerate-shift-to-open-banking/); [Fed SR withdrawal note [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230808a.htm); later status from law-firm and trade sources [S].
7. **Instant payments went mainstream.** FedNow launched (20 Jul) with 35 banks and credit unions. The EU agreed mandatory instant euro transfers at no extra cost, with payee-name checks (7 Nov). Pix handled about 42bn transactions in 2023, more than cards, boletos and TED combined. UPI processed 11–12bn transactions a month by late 2023. VERIFIED: [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20230720a.htm); [EPRS [F]](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2024/757630/EPRS_ATA(2024)757630_EN.pdf); [Agência Brasil [F]](https://agenciabrasil.ebc.com.br/economia/noticia/2024-03/pix-foi-o-meio-de-pagamento-mais-popular-do-brasil-em-2023).
8. **Fraud became the main consumer harm in payments.** US: FBI IC3 recorded $12.5bn in losses (+22%), with crypto-investment fraud at $3.96bn (+53%). FTC: >$10bn, with bank transfers and crypto ahead of all other payment methods combined. The UK made receiving banks share APP-fraud refunds 50:50 for the first time. VERIFIED: [IC3 [F]](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2023_IC3Report.pdf); [FTC [F]](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/02/nationwide-fraud-losses-top-10-billion-2023-ftc-steps-efforts-protect-public); [PSR [F]](https://www.psr.org.uk/news-and-updates/latest-news/news/psr-continues-to-take-bold-action-on-app-fraud-as-it-publishes-final-reimbursement-details-ahead-of-2024-implementation/).
9. **Nigeria ran a natural experiment in forced cash withdrawal, and it was mostly harmful.** Currency in circulation fell from about ₦3.3tn to ₦982bn. Q1 GDP growth fell to 2.3%. Bank apps failed while OPay and Moniepoint gained users. The Supreme Court halted the policy. In June the CBN unified the exchange rate (₦462 to about ₦700/$). VERIFIED: [Carnegie [F]](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/01/why-nigerias-controversial-naira-redesign-policy-hasnt-met-its-objectives?lang=en); [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/03/28/naira-redesign-a-torturous-vanity-project-comes-to-an-end/); [TechCabal FX [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/06/14/cbn-floats-the-naira/).
10. **Fintech funding halved and the BaaS model began to crack.** Global fintech funding was $39.2bn (−50%) on 3,801 deals, the fewest since 2017. Africa VC fell 54% in H1. Supervisors' 2023 exams of partner banks (Evolve, Cross River, Lineage) foreshadowed the 2024 Synapse collapse. VERIFIED: [CB Insights [F]](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/fintech-trends-2023/); [Fed Evolve order [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/enforcement20240614a.htm); [Banking Dive [F]](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/5-lessons-learned-from-synapses-collapse/731543/).

## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 US regional bank crisis (Mar–Jul 2023)

**Timeline (VERIFIED).**
- **8 Mar:** Silvergate announced a voluntary wind-down after FTX-driven deposit flight. Its SEN payments network had closed on 3 Mar. [S&P [S]](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2023/3/silvergate-capital-to-wind-down-operations-liquidate-silvergate-bank-74689105).
- **10 Mar:** SVB failed.
- **12 Mar:** Signature failed. Treasury invoked the systemic-risk exception, which guaranteed all deposits at both banks. The Fed created the Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP): one-year loans against collateral **valued at par**, backed by up to $25bn from Treasury's Exchange Stabilization Fund. [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230312a.htm).
- **19 Mar:** Flagstar (NYCB) took Signature's deposits, excluding about $4bn of digital-asset deposits. Signature had $88.6bn in deposits and $110.4bn in assets. Estimated DIF cost: ~$2.5bn. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23021.html).
- **26 Mar:** First Citizens bought about $72bn of SVB assets at a $16.5bn discount and took on about $119bn of deposits. About $90bn of assets stayed with the FDIC. Estimated DIF cost: ~$20bn. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23023.html).
- **1 May:** First Republic failed ($229.1bn assets, $103.9bn deposits). JPMorgan acquired it with FDIC loss-share. Estimated DIF cost: ~$13bn. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23034.html).
- **25 Jul:** PacWest agreed an all-stock merger into Banc of California, with $400m of new equity from Warburg Pincus and Centerbridge and a plan to repay about $13bn of wholesale borrowings. [Business Wire [S]](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20230725870171/en/Banc-of-California-and-PacWest-Announce-Transformational-Merger-and-400-Million-Equity-Raise-from-Warburg-Pincus-and-Centerbridge).

**Causes.**
- **Growth outpaced controls.** From 2019 to 2021, SVB's assets grew 198% and Signature's 134%, against a 33% peer median. [GAO [F]](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106736).
- **Concentrated, uninsured, networked depositors** (VC-backed tech firms; crypto firms at Signature and Silvergate).
- **Long-duration securities funded by flighty deposits.** SVB triggered its own run on 8 Mar. It announced a $21bn AFS securities sale, a $1.8bn after-tax loss and a $2.25bn capital raise. [BCBS [F]](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d555.htm).
- **Supervision was slow.** The Fed rated SVB satisfactory until 2022, downgraded it in June 2022, and never finalised an enforcement action. The FDIC did not substantially downgrade Signature until the day before it failed. [GAO [F]](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106736). The FDIC admitted examiner staffing shortfalls. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23033.html).
- **Rule changes made it worse.** The Fed's own review faulted the 2018 EGRRCPA "tailoring" changes and a shift to a "less assertive supervisory approach". [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230428a.htm).
- **First Republic** died of contagion layered on interest-rate risk and uninsured-deposit concentration. The BCBS estimates it lost about 37% of deposits in two business days in March. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23073.html); [BCBS [F]](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d555.htm).

**Speed and social media (VERIFIED).**
- The Fed review: on 9 Mar SVB lost >$40bn and management expected >$100bn more on 10 Mar, about 85% of deposits. WaMu lost $19bn over 16 days and Wachovia $10bn over 8 days. [Fed review PDF [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf).
- The BCBS: digitalisation and multi-channel social media (public platforms, encrypted chats, corporate messaging) "are removing many of the frictions" that used to slow outflows. [BCBS [F]](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d555.htm).
- Small banks lost about $108–120bn of deposits in the week to 15 Mar, while large banks gained. Fed H.8 data via [Reuters/Business Standard [S]](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/international/small-us-banks-see-record-drop-in-deposits-after-svb-collapse-fed-data-123032500447_1.html) and [Axios [S]](https://www.axios.com/2023/03/27/small-bank-deposits-fall-but-not-by-much).

**Costs and follow-up.**
- **Special assessment:** $16.3bn on 114 banking organisations (48 over $50bn in assets; none under $5bn), at 13.4bp a year for eight quarters, first invoiced 28 Jun 2024. [FDIC fact sheet [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/fact-sheets/special-assessment-final-rule-11-07-23.pdf).
- **Deposit insurance options (1 May):** the FDIC set out limited, unlimited and targeted coverage and preferred higher limits for business payment accounts. This needs Congress. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23035.html).
- **BTFP:** advances were about $74bn by 19 Apr [GAO [F]](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106736). In Jan 2024 the BTFP rate was floored at the interest rate on reserve balances to stop arbitrage, and new loans ended on 11 Mar 2024. [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20240124a.htm).
- **Silvergate (2024):** paid $63m to the Fed and California DFPI (the SEC's $50m penalty was offset against these payments). Its automated monitoring had skipped more than $1tn of SEN transactions, and it missed about $9bn of suspicious FTX transfers. [Yahoo/CoinDesk [S]](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/crypto-friendly-silvergate-bank-pays-200300639.html).
- **UK spillover:** HSBC bought SVB UK for £1 on 13 Mar (loans about £5.5bn, deposits about £6.7bn). [HSBC [F]](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-media/media-releases/2023/hsbc-acquires-silicon-valley-bank-uk-limited). About 3,000 UK tech firms were at risk. [Fortune commentary [F]](https://fortune.com/2023/03/17/inside-operation-yeti-uk-tech-silicon-valley-bank-run-survived-svb-collapse-venture-capital-tech-europe-russ-shaw/).

### 2.2 Credit Suisse collapse and UBS takeover (Mar 2023)

**What happened (VERIFIED).**
- Credit Suisse suffered large wealth-management outflows from October 2022. The bank survived those only because FINMA had imposed high liquidity buffers. [BCBS [F]](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d555.htm).
- After SVB failed, its CDS spread hit 1,082bp on 16 Mar. CS said it would draw up to CHF 50bn of emergency liquidity (ELA).
- On 19 Mar the Swiss authorities arranged the UBS takeover for about CHF 3bn [S], using emergency law:
  - ELA+ liquidity of up to CHF 100bn;
  - a CHF 100bn public liquidity backstop;
  - a CHF 9bn loss guarantee, after UBS absorbed the first CHF 5bn (UBS ended the guarantee on 8 Aug 2023);
  - no shareholder votes.
- Peak ELA+ use was about CHF 169bn. [BCBS [F]](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d555.htm).
- FINMA ordered "a complete write-down of the nominal value of all AT1 debt", roughly CHF 16bn, while shareholders still received UBS shares. [FINMA [F]](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2023/03/20230319-mm-cs-ubs/); [Business Standard [S]](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/credit-suisse-s-17-bn-of-risky-bonds-are-now-worthless-after-ubs-takeover-123032000091_1.html).

**Why it happened.** FINMA's December 2023 lessons report blames:
- weak strategy, repeated scandals and risk-management failures;
- the weakest capitalisation sitting at the parent company;
- a "digital bank run".

FINMA had run 108 on-site reviews in 2018–22 and raised 382 action points. It asked for a senior managers regime and the power to fine. [FINMA [F]](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2023/12/20231219-mm-cs-bericht/).

**Later developments (dated):**
- **Dec 2024:** the Swiss parliamentary inquiry (PUK, 569 pages) blamed "years of mismanagement". It found no causal misconduct by the authorities but criticised secrecy and FINMA's capital relief. Bonuses paid in 2010–22 exceeded the CHF 34bn of losses. [Daily Sabah/Reuters [F]](https://www.dailysabah.com/business/finance/credit-suisse-inquiry-blasts-oversight-failings-blames-bosses).
- **Oct 2025:** the Federal Administrative Court ruled the AT1 write-down order unlawful. The decision is dated 1 Oct and was published 14 Oct. FINMA is appealing. [DLA Piper [S]](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/11/swiss-court-strikes-down-at1-bond-write-off); [Insurance Journal [S]](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2025/10/15/843793.htm).
- **Status as of Nov 2026: DISPUTED/unresolved.** I could not confirm a Federal Supreme Court outcome.

### 2.3 Rates, margins, deposit migration and commercial real estate

**Rates.**
- The Fed's last hike took the target range to **5.25–5.50%** on 26 Jul 2023. [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230726a.htm).
- The ECB's deposit rate reached **4.00%** on 14 Sep 2023. [ECB [F]](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2023/html/ecb.mp230914~aab39f8c21.en.html).

**Profitability (VERIFIED).**
- US bank net interest margin (NIM) peaked early, then compressed as deposit costs caught up: 3.31% in Q1 (+77bp year on year), 3.28% in Q2, 3.28% in Q4.
- Full-year 2023 net income was **$256.9bn** (−2.3%). Q4 fell to $38.4bn, partly because of the special assessment accrual (INTERPRETATION).
- Domestic deposits fell for five straight quarters to Q2. Uninsured deposits fell **$180.6bn** in Q2 alone. Deposits rose again in Q4 2023 for the first time in seven quarters.
- Sources: [FDIC Q1 [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23043.html), [Q2 [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23072.html), [Q4 [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2024/pr24014.html).

**Migration to money-market funds (REPORTED, snippet-level).**
- MMF assets hit a record **~$5.9tn** (6 Dec 2023, ICI series) [Crane Data [S]](https://cranedata.com/archives/all-articles/9778).
- Inflows were about $1.2tn in 2023 (+25%) [BofA via Investing.com [S]](https://m.uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/equities-posted-the-largest-8week-inflow-since-march-2022--bofa-432SI-3271072?ampMode=1).
- The Fed flagged MMFs and stablecoins as still run-prone. [Fed FSR May 2023 [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2023-may-financial-stability-report-overview.htm).

**Commercial real estate.**
- Noncurrent non-farm, non-residential CRE loans drove the rise in the noncurrent rate: 0.76% in Q2 and 0.86% in Q4. [FDIC [F]](https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2024/pr24014.html).
- Regulators issued a CRE workout and accommodation policy statement on 29 Jun 2023, replacing the 2009 guidance. [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230629a.htm).
- INTERPRETATION: 2023 was mostly a "recognition delay" year for CRE. NYCB/Flagstar, which absorbed Signature, became the first big CRE casualty in early 2024 (an evolution beyond this slice; I did not fetch primary sources for the 2024 losses).

### 2.5 Enforcement and litigation

- **Binance (21 Nov):** the largest settlement in US Treasury history. FinCEN imposed $3.4bn and OFAC $968m (about $4.37bn combined), announced alongside separate DOJ and CFTC resolutions. Findings: more than 100,000 suspicious transactions never reported (including flows tied to Hamas, ISIS and ransomware groups) and 1.67m trades with sanctioned jurisdictions. Binance accepted a five-year monitor and must exit the US. VERIFIED: [Treasury [F]](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1925). (The DOJ page was blocked to my fetch.)
- **FTX/SBF:** convicted on seven counts in Nov 2023. Prosecutors said up to $8bn was taken from customers. Sentenced to 25 years on 28 Mar 2024. [NBC [F]](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/sam-bankman-fried-sentenced-25-years-prison-orchestrating-ftx-fraud-rcna145286).
- **Coinbase:** sued by the SEC on 6 Jun 2023 for operating as an unregistered exchange, broker and clearing agency, and over its staking programme. [SEC [F]](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2023-102).
- **JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank / Epstein:**
  - JPMorgan agreed $290m with victims (12 Jun 2023) over accounts held 1998–2013 [NPR [F]](https://www.npr.org/2023/06/12/1181675580/epstein-jane-doe-1-290-million-settlement-jpmorgan-chase).
  - Deutsche Bank agreed $75m (May 2023) [NPR [F]](https://www.npr.org/2023/06/12/1181675580/epstein-jane-doe-1-290-million-settlement-jpmorgan-chase).
  - JPMorgan agreed $75m with the US Virgin Islands (Sep 2023) [AP title only [S]](https://apnews.com/article/jpmorgan-epstein-virgin-islands-lawsuit-b33f0094586fd4fa229bd6295a7d09e8).
- **Deutsche Bank:** fined $186m by the Fed (19 Jul) for slow sanctions/AML remediation, including weaknesses tied to its relationship with Danske's Estonian branch. [Fed [F]](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/enforcement20230719a.htm). Danske itself pleaded guilty in Dec 2022 [DOJ title [S]](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/danske-bank-pleads-guilty-fraud-us-banks-multi-billion-dollar-scheme-access-us-financial).
- **Wells Fargo:** entered 2023 under the Dec 2022 CFPB order ($2bn+ redress, $1.7bn penalty), which covered surprise overdraft fees and wrongful account freezes. [CFPB [F]](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/wells-fargo-bank-na-2022/).
- **Apple Card / Goldman:**
  - 2023 context: the 4.15% APY savings account launched 17 Apr 2023, and Goldman's card losses were reported above $1bn. [Wikipedia [F]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Card).

### 2.8 Africa and emerging markets

**Nigeria: naira redesign cash crunch (Dec 2022 to Mar 2023)**
- **What happened.** New ₦200/500/1,000 notes went into circulation on 15 Dec 2022, with old notes due to expire (deadlines 31 Jan, then 10 Feb) just before the 25 Feb election. Banks capped withdrawals.
  - Currency in circulation fell from about ₦3.3tn to ₦982bn (Feb 2023). [Carnegie [F]](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/01/why-nigerias-controversial-naira-redesign-policy-hasnt-met-its-objectives?lang=en).
  - Protests spread to Ibadan, Benin, Warri, Abeokuta and Kano, and bank branches were burned. [Africanews [F]](https://www.africanews.com/2023/02/16/currency-crisis-in-nigeria-citizens-take-to-the-streets-in-protest-over-cash-shortage/).
  - The Supreme Court found insufficient notice, and the CBN reversed course in March. Old notes were later declared legal tender "ad infinitum" (Nov 2023). [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/03/28/naira-redesign-a-torturous-vanity-project-comes-to-an-end/); [Carnegie [F]].
- **Effects.**
  - Q1 GDP growth fell to 2.3% (from 3.5%). Nigerian Breweries' Q1 revenue fell 29.7%. CPPE estimated failed and reversed transactions cost ~₦20tn.
  - POS agents charged cash premiums of up to 20%. Bank apps and USSD buckled, and failed transfers took up to 10 days to reverse.
  - OPay (~26m downloads) and other fintechs gained trust. [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/03/13/in-the-midst-of-nigerias-digital-banking-struggles-opay-is-rising-to-the-occasion/); [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/02/22/customers-turn-to-digital-banks/).
  - The eNaira stayed marginal: about 14,000 transactions a week, with 98.5% of wallets idle (IMF). [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/07/13/enaira-blockchain-currency-low-adoption/).

**Nigeria: FX unification (14 Jun 2023)**
- The CBN moved to a managed float. The official rate went from ₦462 to ~₦700 per dollar, with ₦920 on the parallel market by September. [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/06/14/cbn-floats-the-naira/); [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/09/05/cbn-clear-fx-backlog/).
- The FX backlog was ~$10bn, and airlines had ~$812m trapped.
- Remittances could now be paid in naira at market rates, which pulled banks into competition with remittance fintechs. [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/07/20/nigerias-new-fx-regime-has-birthed-fresh-competition-between-banks-and-remittance-startups/).
- Startups that had raised dollars and earned naira saw their runway shrink.

**Nigeria: fraud and the bank–fintech fight (Q4 2023)**
- Fidelity Bank blocked transfers to Kuda, OPay, Moniepoint and PalmPay for about two weeks over KYC concerns. Nigerian institutions have reported ₦159bn in fraud losses since 2020. [TechCabal [F]](https://techcabal.com/2023/10/30/banks-and-neobanks-fraud-problems/).

## 3. Contradictions and disputed claims

- **Size of the SVB run:** CNBC (Barr testimony) says $142bn was ~81% of $175bn year-end deposits [F]. The Fed review says ~85% of the deposit base [F]. The difference is the denominator (year-end vs. the day of failure). DISPUTED in precision only.
- **Small-bank outflow, week to 15 Mar:** $119–120bn (seasonally adjusted, Reuters/Bloomberg) vs $108–109bn (not seasonally adjusted, Axios/Moody's) [S]. Axios framed it as "not by much" relative to total deposits.
- **Did Nigeria's cash crunch boost digital payments?** TechCabal (Mar 2023) reports mobile transactions +125% in Jan and +121% in Feb [F]. TechCabal's own NIBSS analysis (Aug 2023) shows e-payment *value* falling 7.5% in Jan and 5.12% in Feb while *volume* rose, with a record in March (₦48.3tn, 1.18bn transactions), and calls the early-2023 dip seasonally normal [F]. DISPUTED: more people transacted digitally, but less money moved. The 20% POS cash premium and the CPPE's ₦20tn estimate of failed transactions are single-source estimates.
- **The AT1 write-down:** FINMA said the contracts and the emergency ordinance justified it [F]. A Swiss court held in 2025 that it had no legal basis [S]. Unresolved.
- **The size of Credit Suisse's AT1:** reported as "roughly CHF 16bn" (FINMA), CHF 15.8bn, CHF 16.5bn (court) and "$17bn" (press). Different bases and currencies.
- **"No bailout":** officials stressed that SVB and Signature shareholders were wiped out. But uninsured depositors were made whole ($16.3bn cost), the BTFP lent against collateral at par rather than market value, and Switzerland guaranteed CHF 100bn+. INTERPRETATION: this was a depositor and creditor bailout funded by industry levies and state guarantees.
- **CFPB rules:** each rule was presented in 2023 as a fix for a structural harm. By 2025 most were withdrawn, vacated or nullified (late fees, payment apps, AI circular archived, Apple order terminated). The harms the CFPB described were not disproved; the policy direction changed.

## 4. How 2023 issues evolved

- **Earlier (2022):** ultra-low rates and deposit surges set the duration trap. FTX's collapse (Nov 2022) triggered Silvergate's run. CS outflows began in Oct 2022. Nigeria's redesign was announced in Oct 2022. Wells Fargo and Danske had their 2022 resolutions.
- **Later:**
  - BTFP ended in Mar 2024. The special assessment was collected from 2024.
  - CRE losses hit NYCB/Flagstar in 2024.
  - Synapse collapsed (Apr 2024) and Evolve was sanctioned (Jun 2024). Silvergate settled (Jul 2024).
  - UK APP refunds went live (Oct 2024).
  - The PUK report (Dec 2024) and the AT1 court ruling (Oct 2025).
  - US deregulation (2025): CRA repeal of the payments rule, late-fee vacatur, 1033 rewrite, SR letters withdrawn, softer Basel re-proposal (Mar 2026).
  - UPI reached ~18bn transactions a month in 2025. Moniepoint became a unicorn (Oct 2024).
- **INTERPRETATION:** the 2023 crisis produced a burst of tightening proposals. The political cycle then reversed most US consumer and fintech measures before they took effect. EU and UK payment-fraud and instant-payment rules stuck.

## 5. Implications for PayPal and the wider industry

- **PayPal:** INTERPRETATION, grounded in [F] sources.
  1. *Regulatory perimeter:* the nullified CFPB rule would have put PayPal/Venmo under federal supervision. Its removal lowers near-term compliance cost but leaves a reputational gap that state regulators or a future CFPB could fill.
  2. *Fraud:* the UK 50:50 receiving-PSP liability and EU payee-name checks shift fraud costs onto the PSPs that receive money, including wallets.
  3. *Instant rails* (FedNow, RTP, Pix, UPI, SEPA Instant) commoditise "instant transfer" fees and wallet float.
  4. *Stablecoins:* PYUSD launched into a market reset by Binance, FTX and Silvergate. MiCA and the Fed's (later withdrawn) stablecoin process show how fragile regulatory clarity is.
  5. *Engagement:* FY23 active accounts fell 2% even as TPV grew 13% [F]. The growth model was shifting from more users to more transactions per user (58.7 per account).
- **Industry:**
  - Uninsured, concentrated, tech-savvy deposit bases are now treated as a liquidity risk that can move at app speed. This raises the value of insured-deposit sweep networks and of diversified retail funding.
  - Partner-bank BaaS needs proper ledgering and reconciliation.
  - Emerging-market FX shocks (Nigeria, Egypt, Ghana) can wipe out dollar-funded fintech economics.

## 6. Unresolved problems as of Nov 2026 (evidence-backed)

1. **Deposit-insurance design:** the FDIC's preferred targeted coverage for business payment accounts (May 2023) still needs Congress. I found no evidence of enactment (gap noted). [FDIC [F]]
2. **US open-banking rights:** the 1033 rule is enjoined and being rewritten. Bank data-access fees (JPMorgan–Plaid) are being set privately. [S]
3. **Payment-app supervision gap:** the CFPB is barred from re-issuing a "substantially the same" rule after the CRA vote. [S]
4. **Credit Suisse AT1 legality and compensation:** pending appeal. [S]
5. **BaaS end-user protection:** Synapse's $65–95m shortfall exposed the lack of a regulatory ledger or recordkeeping standard. Supervisory follow-through (the Fed's novel-activities programme) was withdrawn in 2025. [F]
6. **Authorised push payment and investment fraud:** US losses keep rising (IC3 +22% in 2023), and the US has no mandatory reimbursement regime comparable to the UK's. [F]
7. **Nigeria:** banks still block or delay transfers to fintechs over KYC without clear rules, and fraud losses are rising. The eNaira failed. [F]
8. **Ghana's banking system:** NPLs at 24.1% (mid-2024), and the central bank itself needs recapitalising after the DDEP. [F]
9. **Mobile-money concentration in Kenya** (M-Pesa 96.5%) and **UPI duopoly** caps keep being deferred. [F]

## 7. Opportunity signals (SPECULATIVE)

1. **Deposit-run early-warning for banks and treasurers.** Real-time monitoring of concentrated depositors, social sentiment and outflow velocity, packaged for mid-sized banks and the VC treasuries of their clients. *Evidence:* the Barr review and BCBS on social-media-driven speed. In Nigeria, a version for MFBs and fintechs facing bank blocks.
2. **Insured-deposit sweeps and treasury management for startups and SMEs**, spreading balances across banks to stay under insurance limits. *Evidence:* the SVB concentration, the 3,000 UK firms at risk, and a $16.3bn uninsured-protection bill that will not recur by default.
3. **BaaS ledger and reconciliation infrastructure** (per-end-user sub-ledgers, daily three-way reconciliation, regulator-readable records). *Evidence:* Synapse's $219m in FBO accounts and $65–95m shortfall, and the Evolve and Lineage orders.
4. **Payee verification and APP-fraud tooling for receiving PSPs.** Confirmation-of-payee, mule-account detection and fraud-data sharing. *Evidence:* the UK 50:50 receiving liability, the EU IBAN/name check, the IC3 crypto-investment surge. *Nigeria angle:* a shared KYC and fraud-signal utility between banks and fintechs, so banks have no pretext for Fidelity-style blocks. Evidence: ₦159bn in fraud losses and the gap in the CDD regulations.
5. **Interbank failure resolution and reversal-tracking for Nigerian payments.** Failed transfers took up to 10 days to resolve during the crunch, and the ecosystem depends on the weakest bank's infrastructure (OPay's own comment). A "failed-transaction concierge" or a smart-routing switch that picks the most reliable rail.
6. **FX-risk and naira-revenue hedging for African startups** (naira-denominated cost structures, local-currency debt, remittance inflows priced at market rates). *Evidence:* ₦462 to ₦700–920, Paystack's offshore cuts, and unification opening market-rate remittances.
7. **Agent-network cash logistics.** During cash shocks, POS agents charged up to 20% cash premiums. A platform matching cash-rich merchants (fuel stations, wholesalers) with agents could narrow the spread. *Evidence:* TechCabal, Carnegie.
8. **Instant-payment overlays** (request-to-pay, pay-by-bank checkout) on FedNow, Pix, UPI and SEPA Instant, since EU rules force price parity and the US has two instant rails. *Evidence:* EPRS, FedNow, the Pix/UPI scale.
9. **Compliance-grade GenAI** (source-grounded assistants like Morgan Stanley's; chatbots that escalate to humans, per CFPB; explainable adverse-action notices). *Evidence:* the 2023 CFPB and BoE statements.

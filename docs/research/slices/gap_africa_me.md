## 2. Findings by country / region

### 2.1 Egypt

**2023 – InstaPay's first year.** CBE reported 20.3m transactions worth EGP 112.7bn for 2.16m customers in InstaPay's first year (article dated 27 Mar 2023). VERIFIED (official figure via [Amwal Al Ghad][F](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=131963)). Also in 2023, CBE set its digital-bank licensing rules: minimum paid-up capital of EGP 2bn (EGP 4bn to lend to large corporates), and the largest shareholder must be a financial institution holding at least 30% ([Scene Now][F](https://scenenow.com/Business/CBE-Issues-Regulations-for-Licensing-of-Digital-Banks)).

**2024 – Float, IMF deal, rail at scale.** At an unscheduled MPC meeting on 6 Mar 2024, CBE raised rates by 600bp (overnight deposit 27.25%, lending 28.25%) and let the pound float ([Egyptian Streets][F](https://egyptianstreets.com/2024/03/06/egypt-devalues-currency-introduces-flexible-exchange-rate/)). The pound fell to about EGP 52/US$ that afternoon, its lowest official level ever, and Egypt finalised an IMF programme enlarged from US$3bn to US$8bn ([The National][F](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2024/03/06/egypts-pound-hits-record-low-after-devaluation)). VERIFIED. In early Nov 2026 the pound traded around 55.3–55.6/US$, from a ticker on the Amwal page ([F], incidental). So the post-float level has broadly held rather than kept sliding (INTERPRETATION).

Over 2024 the instant payment system handled about 1.5bn transactions worth up to EGP 2.9trn, with about 12.5m users. CBE said it was studying InstaPay fees for 2025 ([Egypt Independent][F](https://www.egyptindependent.com/cbe-extends-exemption-for-individuals-on-transfer-fees-via-instapay/)). Fees began on 1 Apr 2025, and InstaPay reportedly turned profitable a year later [S].

**2025 – Wallets and tokenised cards.** By June 2025 InstaPay had more than 16m users and over 1.1bn transactions worth EGP 2.4trn. E-wallets reached 55.5m. Apple Pay, launched in Dec 2024, logged 40m transactions worth EGP 32bn in six months. CBE and I-Score are building an AI credit-scoring model on alternative data ([Daily News Egypt][F](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=840854)). VERIFIED (CBE assistant governor speech).

For telco wallets, NTRA reports 46.3m wallets in Q2 2025, up 29% year on year, and EGP 943.4bn of value. Vodafone Cash has 55% of wallets and 78% of transactions, and 65% of wallet cash-in comes from bank accounts via InstaPay ([TechAfrica News][F](https://techafricanews.com/2025/09/12/egypt-accelerates-financial-inclusion-with-record-e-wallet-adoption-in-2025)). REPORTED: some numbers in that article are garbled, so use only the figures listed here. Banque Misr's onebank received approval in Aug 2025 to become Egypt's first fully digital bank ([Egyptian Streets][F](https://egyptianstreets.com/2025/08/19/central-bank-approves-misr-digital-innovations-transition-to-onebank-egypts-first-fully-digital-bank/)).

**2026.** CBE Decision 106/2026 granted Onebank S.A.E. its digital-bank licence ([EgyptInnovate][F](https://egyptinnovate.com/en/news/the-central-bank-grants-one-bank-the-first-license-to-operate-digital-banking-in-egypt)). CBE's Datahub puts financial inclusion at 79% in June 2026, up 229% from 2016 ([CBE][F](https://www.cbe.org.eg/en/financial-inclusion/data-measurement-and-reports)). VERIFIED.

### 2.2 South Africa

**PayShap (2023–2026).** Volume went from 36,631 payments in its launch month (Mar 2023) to 44.8m a month by Aug 2025, as participating banks rose from 4 to 13. QR payments are planned for 2026 ([The Outlier][F](https://theoutlier.co.za/charts/chart/d83e9360-7de9-4090-a507-554f407cd2e9)). Cumulative transactions went from 461m (worth R403bn, Dec 2025) to 905m with 6m users (May 2026).

PayInc now says openly that "reducing the cost of doing payments and ensuring seamless, consistent user experiences across banking channels" are prerequisites. It is shifting PayShap toward merchants, where Yoco, SnapScan, Zapper, Ozow and the card schemes are already established. SARB's 50% stake and the 2026 budget's designation of PayInc as shared national infrastructure change the incentives of the bank-owned operator ([TechCentral][F](https://techcentral.co.za/three-years-in-payshap-pivots-to-merchants/281687/)). VERIFIED.

**Two-pot retirement system (from Sep 2024).** About R43bn had been withdrawn by early 2025 ([Daily Maverick][F](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-04-03-avoid-the-double-whammy-of-two-pot-withdrawals/)). SARS collected R12.9bn in tax on the withdrawals in 2024/25, against R5bn projected, plus R820m in recovered tax debt. Sanlam alone paid 162,000 claims worth R2.9bn ([Moonstone][F](https://www.moonstone.co.za/groceries-gadgets-and-gaps-where-two-pot-savings-are-really-going/)). VERIFIED. A later figure of R57bn by mid-2025 is [S] only. INTERPRETATION: two-pot was a one-off household liquidity injection, and it shows how stretched SA consumers are. It overlaps with the rise in fraud and credit complaints.

**Fraud and complaints.** The NFO banking division (the former Ombudsman for Banking Services) opened a record 15,412 cases in 2024, against 8,521 in 2023. 79% of formal findings favoured the bank. Capitec (1,203 cases) and FNB (1,017) led ([Moonstone][F](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fraud-floods-complaints-as-banking-ombud-speeds-up-resolutions/)). In 2025 fraud complaints almost doubled and 3,651 fraud cases were closed. The main types were mobile-banking fraud, phishing, vishing, push-payment scams and card-not-present fraud, with mule accounts spread across banks ([The Citizen][F](https://citizen.co.za/business/fraud-complaints-at-the-banking-ombud-nearly-double-in-a-year-as-digital-scams-surge)).

SABRIC 2024: total financial-crime losses fell 18% from R3.3bn to R2.7bn. Digital-banking fraud, however, was 65.3% of incidents. CNP accounts for 85.6% of SA-issued credit-card fraud losses, and AI and voice-deepfake scams are appearing ([SecuritySA][F](https://www.securitysa.com/25832r)). SAPS data show commercial crime still rising every quarter ([Daily Maverick][F](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-03-22-how-commercial-crime-quietly-became-sas-most-dangerous-growth-industry/)). See §3 for the dispute over SABRIC's digital-fraud figure.

FSCA had licensed about 300 crypto-asset service providers by Dec 2025 (75 in Apr 2024, 248 in Dec 2024) [S only; the IOL and Cover pages returned 403].

### 2.3 Ghana

**E-Levy repeal (2025).** Parliament passed the repeal on 26 Mar 2025. The levy had started at 1.75% in 2022 and was cut to 1%, and it applied to MoMo, bank transfers and inward remittances. The deputy minister said repeal "returns GH¢2bn" to citizens ([GNA][F](https://gna.org.gh/2025/03/parliament-abolishes-e-levy-returns-gh2-billion-to-ghanaians/)). Assent came on 2 Apr 2025. GRA ordered deductions to stop at midnight and refunds to be paid. MTN's CEO: "If I do it ahead of time, the Bank of Ghana will catch me" ([Graphic][F](https://www.graphic.com.gh/business/business-news/mtn-ghana-abolishes-1-e-levy-as-new-tax-reforms-take-effect.html)). VERIFIED.

In 2025, the first full year without the levy, MoMo value rose 50.8% to GH¢4.54trn, and GhIPSS Instant Pay value doubled to GH¢712bn (BoG 2025 Systems Oversight Report via [Ghanaian Times][F](https://ghanaiantimes.com.gh/mobile-money-transactions-hit-gh¢4-54trn-in-2025-bog/)). The increase is VERIFIED; attributing it to the repeal is INTERPRETATION, since cedi recovery and inflation also played a part. Bank transfer charges came back after the repeal, and in May 2026 BoG paused MTN MMFL's proposed 0.75% wallet-to-bank fee [S].

**DDEP aftermath (2023–2026).** The IMF says 13 banks with post-DDEP capital shortfalls had met their recapitalisation requirements by end-2024; one state-owned bank lags. The NPL ratio was 22.6% in 2024, and specialised deposit-taking institutions remain undercapitalised ([Citi Newsroom][F](https://citinewsroom.com/2025/07/13-banks-meet-recapitalisation-targets-but-one-state-owned-lags-behind-imf/)). By 2026 the IMF notes that bank profitability has been helped by high-yield BoG bills, with private-sector NPLs at 20.8% ([MyJoyOnline][F](https://www.myjoyonline.com/banking-sector-records-gradual-recovery-but-npls-sovereign-exposures-remain-high-imf/)).

The central bank absorbs the cost: negative equity of GH¢93.82bn at end-2025 (GH¢58.62bn in 2024), from DDEP losses and sterilisation operations, with a MoF–BoG recapitalisation MoU covering 2026–2032 ([GNA][F](https://gna.org.gh/2026/05/bog-records-negative-equity-in-2025-following-ddep/)). VERIFIED.

### 2.5 Ethiopia

**Telebirr.** FY2024/25 (ENA): Ethio Telecom revenue was 162bn birr, and the CEO said telebirr "processed 1.66trn birr in customer transactions" ([ENA][F](https://www.ena.et/web/eng/w/eng_7003355)). H1 FY2025/26: 58.61m users, 6.88trn birr cumulative, and more than 16m customers served by Telebirr credit ([StockMarket.et][F](https://www.stockmarket.et/telebirr-transactions-surpass-6-88-trillion-birr-user-base-reaches-58-6-million/)). Full FY2025/26: 60.6m customers, 2.61bn transactions (+145%), 4.19trn birr, 19.51bn birr of collateral-free loans to 5.65m people. Telebirr is still only 3.6% of Ethio Telecom revenue ([Reporter][F](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/52133/)). VERIFIED.

**Macro inclusion.** NBE says digital payments grew more than 750% in five years and now exceed cash by value, and digital accounts rose from 18.6m to over 100m, of which more than 70m are mobile money ([NBE NAFIR][F](https://nbe.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NAFIR.pdf)). Findex account ownership, however, moved only from 46% to 49% (2021→2024), and the gender gap is 57% vs 42%. NBE's register lists only six non-bank payment-instrument-issuer licences ([NBE][F](https://nbe.gov.et/payment-instrument-issuers-system-operators/)).

**Liberalisation.** The Banking Business Proclamation (approved Dec 2024, in force 2025) allows foreign subsidiaries, branches and stakes, with a 40% cap per investor ([Addis Fortune][F](https://addisfortune.news/news-alert/parliament-approves-law-allowing-entry-of-foreign-banks)). By Oct 2026 two foreign banks had applied via greenfield entry and none was operating yet ([Capital Ethiopia][F](https://capitalethiopia.com/2026/10/04/two-foreign-banks-apply-to-enter-ethiopia-through-greenfield-investments/)). VERIFIED.

### 2.8 Nigeria (items not covered by other slices)

## 4. Evolution, 2023 → 2026 (INTERPRETATION)

- **2023:** rails were launched or still young (InstaPay in its first year, PayShap in Mar 2023, Aani in Oct 2023). Ethiopia opened payments to foreign firms (M-Pesa, Aug 2023). Ghana absorbed the DDEP.
- **2024:** shocks and repair. Egypt floated and the IMF programme was upsized. Ghana's banks recapitalised. Nigeria's fraud losses tripled on one insider case. Two-pot opened retirement savings in SA. WAEMU built STAR-UEMOA. Fawran launched in Qatar.
- **2025:** policy reversals and mandates. Ghana's E-Levy was repealed, Kenya's DAT repealed, InstaPay began charging fees, the UAE ordered the OTP phase-out, FCCPC's lending rules were gazetted, PI-SPI launched, Ethiopia's foreign-bank law took effect, and Nigeria's USSD billing moved to airtime.
- **2026:** enforcement and consolidation, plus legal pushback. Wave was brought into PI-SPI, the USSD debt was cleared, FCCPC's rules were suspended by court, SARB took co-ownership of PayInc, Egypt licensed its first digital bank, and BoG began recapitalisation.

## 5. Unresolved problems as of Nov 2026 (evidence-backed)

1. **Bank-owned instant rails are still overpriced.** PayShap's operator admits fees are holding adoption back, and USSD access is still missing ([TechCentral][F](https://techcentral.co.za/three-years-in-payshap-pivots-to-merchants/281687/)). InstaPay moved to fees in 2025 [S]. Ghana's wallet-to-bank fees returned after the levy was repealed [S].
2. **Fraud on banking apps and push payments keeps growing, and banks mostly win the disputes.** In SA 79% of ombud findings favour banks, while fraud complaints doubled. Israel and BoI describe impersonation fraud as surging. BCEAO is fighting deepfakes. Nigeria's 2024 spike was an insider case, a governance failure rather than a consumer-side one.
3. **Digital-lending regulation is unsettled.** Nigeria's DEON rules are suspended by court. Kenya has 227 licensed DCPs out of more than 800 applicants, with many still pending.
4. **Telco PSBs have not repeated M-Pesa.** The lending ban, late entry and OPay/PalmPay's head start are the reasons given. MoMo PSB shrank ([AllBusiness.Africa][F](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/nigeria-psb-three-years-in)).
5. **Account access is outpacing account use and gender equity.** Ethiopia has more than 100m digital accounts but 49% Findex ownership and a 15pp gender gap. Morocco has 13.8m wallets but mobile payments at 0.1% of transactions, with cash up 22%.
6. **Sovereign-debt damage lingers.** BoG's equity is −GH¢93.8bn, and Ghanaian banks remain dependent on sovereign paper with about 21% NPLs.
7. **Cross-border African payments are still at an early stage.** PAPSS covers 19 of 54 countries with no published value data, and USD 846m in airline revenue remains trapped.

## 6. Opportunity signals (all SPECULATIVE)

1. **Fraud intelligence across banks, wallets and telcos.** Mule-account networks across banks are flagged by the NFO, BoI now permits inter-bank fraud information sharing, and NIBSS found losses from static-photo BVNs. This points to a consortium mule-detection and liveness-as-a-service product for Nigerian MFBs, PSBs and wallets, first in Nigeria and then in WAEMU and SA.
2. **OTP replacement in the Gulf, and later Africa.** CBUAE's SMS-OTP deadline (Mar 2026) creates demand for passkey and device-binding SDKs. African regulators often follow Gulf and Singapore precedent. A Lagos team could build passkey and in-app authentication for Nigerian banks before the CBN mandates it.
3. **Building on newly mandated rails.** PI-SPI (175 participants, free to users), PayShap's merchant/QR push, and Treasury bulk payments over PI-SPI all need merchant acceptance tooling, reconciliation, and bulk-disbursement APIs. Nigerian NIP know-how can be exported to WAEMU and SA.
4. **Credit on top of wallets, within the law.** Telebirr (19.5bn birr), Uganda MoMo loans (Shs2.75trn) and Fuliza growth show demand for wallet credit. Nigeria's PSBs cannot lend. That suggests a lending-as-a-service partnership in which licensed lenders underwrite on PSB or wallet data, compliant with FCCPC rules once the court case is resolved.
5. **Complaint and dispute resolution tooling.** CBN complaints are up 10.5% with US$329m in refunds ordered, and the SA ombud caseload doubled. Regulators fine poor complaint handling (N1.69bn). B2B complaint management with regulatory reporting templates is a likely product.
6. **Bridging PAPSS and corridors.** With PAPSS live in 19 countries and the PAPSS–InstaPay link planned [S], a merchant FX and settlement layer for Nigeria–Ghana–Egypt SME trade is a possibility.
7. **Digital-asset compliance.** Nigeria's SEC registers exchanges and a stablecoin offering, Kenya repealed its DAT, and SA has about 300 licensed CASPs [S]. Licensing, Travel-Rule and tax-reporting tooling for VASPs across these regimes is an opening.
